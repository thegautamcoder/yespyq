The silver coin 'tanka' was introduced by __________ .

bIltutmish
cBalban
dBahram Khan
✓ Correct answer: b) Iltutmish
ExplanationOption A: Qutubuddin Aibak → Founder of Slave dynasty, but he did not issue the silver tanka.Option B: Iltutmish → ✔ He introduced the silver tanka (standard coin) and the copper jital.Option C: Balban → Known for strong centralised rule, but not for introducing tanka.Option D: Bahram Khan → Weak ruler, no such contribution.Hence, the correct answer is "Iltutmish ".

aHumayun
bAkbar
cJahangir
dBabur
✓ Correct answer: d) Babur

Q4 FREE PREVIEW
The Chola kingdom mostly ruled which region of India?
aEast
bWest
cNorth
dSouth
✓ Correct answer: d) South
ExplanationThe Chola dynasty governed over a large region of southern India for 400 years, involving the entirety of the present state of Tamil Nadu and nearby regions of southern Karnataka and Andhra.They built a strong navy, that helped them conquering Sri Lanka and the Maldives.They are the longest-ruling dynasties in the history of southern India.The earliest citations to this Tamil dynasty are in inscriptions from the 3rd century BCE left by Ashoka, of the Maurya Empire.Hence, the correct answer is “South”.

✓ Correct answer: d) Shah Jahan

Q8 FREE PREVIEW
Which dynasty came to power in India after the Tughlaq dynasty?
aThe Guptas
bThe Khiljis
cThe Mughals
dThe Sayyids
✓ Correct answer: d) The Sayyids
ExplanationThe Delhi Sultanate was an Islamic kingdom that stretched over large parts of India for 320 years (1206-1526 AD).Five dynasties that ruled as a part Delhi Sultanate are as follows: Mamluk dynasty (1206-1290 AD) Khilji dynasty (1290-1320 AD) Tughlaq dynasty (1320-1414 AD) Sayyid dynasty (1414-1451 AD) Lodi dynasty (1451-1526 AD) The Sayyid dynasty was the fourth dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate.It was ruled by four Sultans from the period 1414 to 1451. Established by the then governor of Multan, Khizr Khan, in 1414 they replaced the Tughlaq dynasty.Hence, The correct answer is "The Sayyids".

Q17 FREE PREVIEW
Who amongst the following was the successor of Sikh Guru Har Krishan?
aGuru Angad Dev
bGuru Teg Bahadur
cGuru Hargobind
dGuru Amar Das
✓ Correct answer: b) Guru Teg Bahadur
ExplanationFrom the late 15th century to the beginning of the 18th century, ten successive Gurus consciously channeled and acknowledged the power of the Shabad Guru.They revolutionized the spiritual, social, economic, and political life of their time.Some of them are: Guru Har Krishan Sahib (1661 to 1664) was installed as a Guru at the age of five years.Guru Teg Bahadur Sahib was his successor.Guru Angad Dev (1539 to 1552): second of the 10 gurus, invented and introduced the Gurmukhi script.Guru Amar Das Sahib (1552 to 1574): introduced the Anand Karaj marriage ceremony for the Sikhs.Guru Har Gobind Sahib (1606 to 1644): He was the son of Guru Arjan Dev.He was known as a "soldier saint” because he organized a small army and became the first Guru to take up arms to defend the faith.Guru Teg Bahadur Sahib (1665 to 1675): He established the town of Anandpur.Hence, the correct answer is “Guru Teg Bahadur”.
Q18 FREE PREVIEW
The Red Fort in Delhi was the residence of emperors of which dynasty in the 16th century?
aRajput
bKhalji
cTughluq
dMughal
✓ Correct answer: d) Mughal
ExplanationThe Mughal dynasty, under Emperor Shah Jahan, constructed the Red Fort in Delhi.Completed in 1648, the Red Fort served as the main residence of the Mughal emperors for nearly 200 years.It was designed to be the centre of Mughal power and governance in the capital city of Shahjahanabad (present- day Old Delhi).The fort's imposing red sandstone walls housed not only the royal residence but also various administrative and ceremonial buildings.Its architectural grandeur and historical significance make it one of the most iconic symbols of Mughal architecture in India.Hence, the correct answer is "Mughal."
