Q14

The sixth mutant codon of beta globin gene causing polymerization of Haemoglobin and change in RBC shape is ______.

[NEET 2026]

a

CAG

b

GUG

c

AUG

d

GAG

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

GUG

Explanation

  • The question refers to sickle cell anaemia, a classical example of a point mutation in the β-globin gene.

  • In normal haemoglobin (HbA), the 6th codon of the β-globin gene is GAG, which codes for the amino acid glutamic acid.

  • In the mutant form (HbS), this codon undergoes a substitution mutation where adenine (A) is replaced by uracil (U) in mRNA, changing the codon to GUG.

  • This results in substitution of valine instead of glutamic acid at the 6th position of the β-chain.

  • The altered haemoglobin (HbS) becomes less soluble and tends to polymerize under low oxygen conditions, causing RBCs to become sickle-shaped.

  • Therefore, the mutant sixth codon responsible for this disorder is GUG (Option B).

Q17

What is the probability of having children with 'O' blood group, where both mother and father are heterozygous for 'A' and 'B' blood group, respectively?

[NEET 2026]

a

50%

b

0%

c

75%

d

25%

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

25%

Explanation

  • Blood group inheritance in humans is governed by the ABO gene with alleles Iᴬ, Iᴮ, and i.

  • A heterozygous A blood group individual has genotype Iᴬi (mother).

  • A heterozygous B blood group individual has genotype Iᴮi (father).

  • When crossed, the possible gametes are:

    • Mother → Iᴬ, i

    • Father → Iᴮ, i

  • Offspring combinations:

    • IᴬIᴮ → AB blood group

    • Iᴬi → A blood group

    • Iᴮi → B blood group

    • ii → O blood group

  • Each combination occurs with equal probability (1/4).

  • Therefore, the probability of having children with blood group O (ii genotype) is 25%.

  • Hence, the correct answer is Option D.

Q25

Genes R and Y follow independent assortment. If RRYY produce round yellow seeds and rryy produce wrinkled green seeds, what will be the phenotypic ratio of the F2 generation?

[NEET 2025]

a

Phenotypic ratio -9:3:3:1

b

Phenotypic ratio -9:7

c

Phenotypic ratio - 1:2:1

d

Phenotypic ratio - 3: 1

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

Phenotypic ratio -9:3:3:1

Explanation

Genes R (round) and Y (yellow) assort independently, meaning they are on different chromosomes or behave independently during gamete formation.

  • Parent generation: RRYY (round yellow) × rryy (wrinkled green)

  • F1 generation: All RrYy (round yellow), due to dominant traits

  • F2 generation: Cross RrYy × RrYy (dihybrid cross)

This gives the classic dihybrid phenotypic ratio:

  • 9 Round Yellow (R–Y–)

  • 3 Round Green (R–yy)

  • 3 Wrinkled Yellow (rrY–)

  • 1 Wrinkled Green (rryy)

So, the phenotypic ratio is 9:3:3:1, which is typical for two genes showing independent assortment.

✓ Correct answer: d)

Explanation

The inheritance pattern of the human ABO blood grouping system is governed by a single gene denoted as 'I', which uniquely possesses three different alleles: IA, IB, and the recessive allele 'i'. Because alleles IA and IB exhibit complete dominance over the recessive allele 'i', a person can only express blood group 'O' if they inherit two copies of the recessive allele, resulting strictly in the homozygous genotype 'ii'.

Consequently, for a child to have blood group 'O', it is an absolute genetic requirement that both parents carry and pass on at least one 'i' allele.
