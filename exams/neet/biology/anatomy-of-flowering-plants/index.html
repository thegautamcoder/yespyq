Pith and cortex do not differentiate in

bdicot stem
cmonocot root
ddicot root.

✓ Correct answer: a) monocot stem
ExplanationIn a monocot stem, the ground tissue is not distinctly differentiated into cortex, endodermis, pericycle, and pith as seen in dicot stems and roots.Instead, it forms a uniform mass of parenchymatous cells.The vascular bundles are scattered throughout the ground tissue and not arranged in a ring.Due to this scattered arrangement and lack of specific organization, distinct pith and cortex regions are absent.In contrast, dicot stems and both monocot and dicot roots show clear differentiation of ground tissues into cortex, endodermis, pericycle, and pith.Hence, monocot stems lack distinct pith and cortex.

Q5
Casparian strip occurs in a:
aEndodermis
bExodermis
cPericycle
dEpidermis
🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a) Endodermis
ExplanationThe correct option is A. Endodermis.The Casparian strip is a distinctive feature found in the endodermis, which is the innermost layer of the cortex in the roots of vascular plants.It is a band-like thickening on the radial and transverse walls of the endodermal cells, composed primarily of a waxy, water-impermeable substance called suberin, and sometimes lignin.The primary function of the Casparian strip is to act as a barrier, blocking the passive flow of water and dissolved solutes through the cell walls (apoplastic pathway) into the vascular cylinder (stele).B. Exodermis: The exodermis is the outer layer of the root cortex and while it also has a role in regulating water flow and protection, it does not typically contain Casparian strips in the same way the endodermis does.C. Pericycle: The pericycle is a layer of cells located just inside the endodermis, surrounding the vascular tissue.It is involved in the formation of lateral roots, but its cells do not have Casparian strips.D. Epidermis: The epidermis is the outermost protective layer of cells in the plant.Its outer walls are often covered with a waxy cuticle (in stems and leaves, not typically in roots), but it does not contain Casparian strips.

Bulliform cells are responsible for

[NEET 2024]

a

Providing large spaces for storage of sugars

b

Inward curling of leaves in monocots

c

Protecting the plant from salt stress

d

Increased photosynthesis in monocots

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

Inward curling of leaves in monocots

Explanation

Bulliform cells are large, vacuolated epidermal cells found in the leaves of monocots, especially grasses. They help in minimizing water loss during drought conditions. When water is abundant, these cells remain turgid, keeping the leaf blade open. However, under water stress, they lose turgor pressure, causing the leaf to fold inward, reducing transpiration. This is an adaptive mechanism to conserve water.

Q13

The main function of bulliform cells in grasses is :

[NEET 2026]

a

to make the leaf impermeable to fungal spores.

b

to perform photosynthesis.

c

to minimize water loss during water stress.

d

to transport water.

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

to minimize water loss during water stress.

Explanation

Bulliform cells (also called motor cells) are large, thin-walled epidermal cells found on the upper surface of grass leaves.

  • Under water stress, these cells lose water and become flaccid, causing the leaf to roll or fold inward.
  • This rolling reduces the exposed surface area and minimizes water loss through transpiration.
  • When sufficient water is available, the cells regain turgidity, and the leaf unrolls.

Thus, their primary role is to help the plant conserve water during unfavorable conditions.

Answer: Option C (to minimize water loss during water stress)

Q16

Which of the following helps in maintenance of the pressure gradient in sieve tubes?

[Re-NEET 2024]

a

Albuminous cells

b

Sieve cells

c

Phloem parenchyma

d

Companion cells

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

Companion cells

Explanation

Correct answer: D — Companion cells

  • Companion cells are closely associated with sieve tube elements in angiosperms.

  • They help in maintaining the pressure gradient needed for translocation of food (photosynthates) through sieve tubes by actively loading and unloading sugars.

  • This maintains the osmotic balance and drives the pressure flow mechanism (Mass Flow Hypothesis).
