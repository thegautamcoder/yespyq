ExplanationThe statement that is not true for glucose is that glucose gives Schiff's test for aldehyde.Here is an analysis of each statement: A. Q12

The protein portion of an enzyme is called :

a

Apoenzyme

b

Prosthetic group

c

Cofactor

d

Coenzyme

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

Apoenzyme

Explanation

The correct answer is Apoenzyme.

An enzyme may consist of two parts: a protein portion and a non-protein component. The protein part alone of an enzyme is called the apoenzyme. By itself, the apoenzyme is usually inactive and cannot catalyse a reaction efficiently. For the enzyme to become fully functional, it often needs a cofactor, which may be a metal ion or an organic molecule. When the apoenzyme combines with its required cofactor, the complete and active enzyme formed is called a holoenzyme.

A prosthetic group is a tightly bound cofactor, while a coenzyme is an organic cofactor that is loosely attached. Thus, among the given options, apoenzyme specifically refers to the protein portion of an enzyme and is the correct answer.

Q18

Name the class of enzyme that usually catalyze the following reaction:

S-G+S# →S+S#-G

Where, G → a group other than hydrogen
S → a substrate
S# → another substrate

[NEET 2025]

a

Transferase

b

Ligase

c

Hydrolase

d

Lyase

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

Transferase

Explanation

The given reaction:
S−G + S# → S + S#−G
shows the transfer of a group (G) from one substrate (S) to another (S#). This type of reaction is catalyzed by transferase enzymes.

Transferases are enzymes that transfer a functional group (like methyl, phosphate, or glycosyl group) from one molecule (donor) to another (acceptor). In this reaction, G (a group other than hydrogen) is transferred from S to S#.

Other options:

  • Ligase joins two molecules.

  • Hydrolase breaks bonds using water.

  • Lyase breaks bonds without water or oxidation.

Thus, the correct answer is A — Transferase.

Q34

Alpha-helix is found in which level of protein structure?

[NEET 2026]

a

Quaternary structure

b

Tertiary structure

c

Primary structure

d

Secondary structure

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

Secondary structure

Explanation

Proteins have different levels of structural organisation:

  • Primary structure: Linear sequence of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
  • Secondary structure: Regular folding of the polypeptide chain into structures like α-helix and β-pleated sheet, stabilized by hydrogen bonds.
  • Tertiary structure: Overall 3D folding of a single polypeptide chain.
  • Quaternary structure: Association of two or more polypeptide chains.

The α-helix is a coiled structure formed due to hydrogen bonding within the polypeptide backbone and is a characteristic feature of the secondary structure.

Answer: Option D (Secondary structure)

Catalase

Explanation

Catalase is an enzyme found in almost all living organisms exposed to oxygen. It helps in breaking down hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), a harmful byproduct of metabolism, into water and oxygen.

Catalase contains ‘haem’ (heme) as a prosthetic group, which means it has an iron-containing porphyrin ring tightly bound to the enzyme. The haem group is essential for the oxidative breakdown of hydrogen peroxide.

Q44

Regarding catalytic cycle of an enzyme action, select the correct sequential steps :
A. Substrate enzyme complex formation.
B. Free enzyme ready to bind with another substrate.
C. Release of products.
D. Chemical bonds of the substrate broken.
E. Substrate binding to active site.

Choose the correct answer from the options given below :

[NEET 2024]

a

E, D, C, A, B

b

E, A, D, C, B

c

A, E, B, D, C

d

B, A, C, D, E

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

E, A, D, C, B

Explanation

The correct sequential steps of the catalytic cycle of enzyme action are:

  1. Substrate binding to active site (v) – The substrate attaches to the enzyme’s active site.
  2. Substrate-enzyme complex formation (i) – The enzyme stabilizes the substrate in the active site.
  3. Chemical bonds of the substrate broken (iv) – The enzyme catalyzes the reaction, leading to bond breaking or formation.
  4. Release of products (iii) – The reaction products are released from the enzyme.
  5. Free enzyme ready to bind with another substrate (ii) – The enzyme returns to its original form to catalyse another reaction.
