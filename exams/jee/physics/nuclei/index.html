Q1 FREE PREVIEW

Two nuclei of mass number 3 combine with another nucleus of mass number 4 to yield a nucleus of mass number 10. If the binding energy per nucleon for the mass numbers 3,4 and 10 are 5.6 MeV, 7.4 MeV and 6.1 MeV, respectively, then in the process, ∆Mc2=____ MeV.

[04 April, 2026 (Shift-I)]

a

6.9

b

7.9

c

2.2

d

4.3

✓ Correct answer: c)

2.2

Explanation

Initial total binding energy is

2×3×5.6+4×7.4=33.6+29.6=63.2MeV

Final binding energy is

10×6.1=61.0MeV

Therefore,

ΔMc2=63.2−61.0=2.2MeV

Q3 FREE PREVIEW

Two radioactive substances A and B of mass numbers 200 and 212 respectively, shows spontaneous α-decay with same Q value of 1 Me V. The ratio of energies of α-rays produced by A and B is __________

[JEE Main 2026, 8 Apr (Shift 2)]

a

25482650

b

27062646

c

25972600

d

28622499

✓ Correct answer: c)

25972600

Explanation

For α-decay, kinetic energy of the α-particle is

Kα=A-4AQ

For substance A with mass number 200:

Kα1=196200Q

For substance B with mass number 212:

Kα2=208212Q

Therefore,

Kα1Kα2=196200·212208=25972600

Q4 FREE PREVIEW

Energy released when two deuterons (H12) fuse to form a helium nucleus (He24) is :
(Given : Binding energy per nucleon of H12=1.1 MeV and binding energy per nucleon of He24=7.0 MeV )

[JEE Main 2025, 2 Apr (Shift 2)]

a

8.1 MeV

b

5.9 MeV 

c

23.6 MeV

d

26.8 MeV

✓ Correct answer: c)

23.6 MeV

Explanation


Binding energy per nucleon of deuteron \( \left(^2_1\text{H}\right) = 1.1\, \text{MeV} \)
Binding energy per nucleon of helium-4 \( \left(^4_2\text{He}\right) = 7.0\, \text{MeV} \)

Two deuterons \( \Rightarrow \) total nucleons = 2 × 2 = 4

\[
\text{BE}_{\text{reactants}} = 2 \times (1.1 \times 2) = 4.4\, \text{MeV}
\]

\[
\text{BE}_{\text{product}} = 4 \times 7.0 = 28.0\, \text{MeV}
\]

Q=BEproduct −BEreactants =28.0−4.4=23.6MeV

Q5 FREE PREVIEW

A radioactive nucleus \(\mathrm{n}_2\) has 3 times the decay constant as compared to the decay constant of another radioactive nucleus \(n_1\). If initial number of both nuclei are the same, what is the ratio of number of nuclei of \(n_2\) to the number of nuclei of \(n_1\), after one half-life of \(n_1\) ?

[JEE Main 2025, 23 Jan (Shift 1)]

a

8

b

4

c

18

d

14

✓ Correct answer: d)

14

Explanation

The number of remaining nuclei after time t can be given by:

N(t)=N0e−λt

For half-life t1/2 of n1, we know that:

t1/2=ln2λ1

At the end of one half-life, the number of n1 nuclei left will be:

N1=N02

For nucleus n2, which has a decay constant 3 times that of n1 the remaining number of nuclei will be:

N2=N0e-3λ1t

Since t=t1/2=ln2λ1, we substitute it into the equation for N2 :

N2=N0e-3λ1×ln2λ1=N0e-3ln2=N0123=N08


Now, the ratio of the number of nuclei of n2 to n1 after one half-life of n1 is:

N2N1=N08N02=14
