Q12

A solid sphere (A) of mass 5m and a spherical shell (B) of mass m, both having same radius, are placed on a rough surface. When a force of same magnitude is applied tangentially at the highest points of A and B, they start rolling without slipping with an acceleration of aA and aB, respectively. The ratio of aA and aB is ____________.

[JEE Main 2026, 6 Apr (Shift 2)]

a

5 : 21

b

6 : 10

c

21 : 25

d

1 : 5

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

5 : 21

Explanation

Let's first derive a general formula for the linear acceleration (\(a\)) of a round body of mass \(M\), radius \(R\), and moment of inertia Icm when a tangential force \(F\) is applied at its highest point.

Assume a static friction force \(f\) acts backward at the point of contact with the rough surface.

Translational Motion (Newton's Second Law):

\[F - f = Ma\]

Rotational Motion (about the Center of Mass):

Both the applied force \(F\) and the friction \(f\) produce a clockwise torque.

\[\tau_{net} = F \cdot R + f \cdot R = I_{cm} \alpha\]

Since the body is rolling without slipping on a stationary surface, we use the constraint a=αR, which gives α=aR. Substituting this in:

\[R(F + f) = I_{cm} \left(\frac{a}{R}\right)\] \[F + f = \frac{I_{cm} a}{R^2}\]

Solving for Acceleration (\(a\)):

Add Equation 1 and Equation 2 to eliminate \(f\):

\[(F - f) + (F + f) = Ma + \frac{I_{cm} a}{R^2}\] \[2F = a \left(M + \frac{I_{cm}}{R^2}\right)\] \[a = \frac{2F}{M + \frac{I_{cm}}{R^2}}\]

Applying to Solid Sphere (A)

Mass:MA=5m

Moment of Inertia: For a solid sphere, I=25MR2.

\[I_A = \frac{2}{5}(5m)R^2 = 2mR^2\]

Acceleration (aA):

\[a_A = \frac{2F}{5m + \frac{2mR^2}{R^2}}\] \[a_A = \frac{2F}{5m + 2m} = \frac{2F}{7m}\]

Applying to Spherical Shell (B)

Mass: MB=m

Moment of Inertia: For a spherical shell (hollow sphere), I=23MR2.

\[I_B = \frac{2}{3}mR^2\]

Acceleration (aB):

\[a_B = \frac{2F}{m + \frac{\frac{2}{3}mR^2}{R^2}}\] \[a_B = \frac{2F}{m + \frac{2}{3}m} = \frac{2F}{\frac{5}{3}m} = \frac{6F}{5m}\]

Finding the Ratio

Now, we find the ratio of aA to aB:

\[\frac{a_A}{a_B} = \frac{\left(\frac{2F}{7m}\right)}{\left(\frac{6F}{5m}\right)}\] \[\frac{a_A}{a_B} = \frac{2F}{7m} \times \frac{5m}{6F}\] \[\frac{a_A}{a_B} = \frac{10}{42} = \frac{5}{21}\]

The ratio of their accelerations is 5 : 21.

aMR2(2at+b)aMR^2 (2at + b)

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

aMR2(2at+b)aMR^2 (2at + b)

Explanation

  1. Expression for Torque and Power: The power derived to the disc is given by:

    P=τ⋅ωP = \tau \cdot \omega

    where τ\tau is the torque, and ω\omega is the angular velocity.

  2. Moment of Inertia: For a disc rotating about its axis:

    I=12MR2I = \frac{1}{2} M R^2

  3. Angular Velocity (ω\omega): Angular velocity is the derivative of angle θ\theta with respect to time:

    ω=dθdt=ddt(at2+bt+c)\omega = \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt} \left( at^2 + bt + c \right), ω=2at+b\omega = 2at + b

  4. Angular Acceleration (α\alpha): Angular acceleration is the derivative of angular velocity:

    α=dωdt=ddt(2at+b)\alpha = \frac{d\omega}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt} \left( 2at + b \right), α=2a\alpha = 2a

  5. Torque (τ\tauτ): Torque is related to angular acceleration:

    τ=I⋅α\tau = I \cdot \alpha

    Substitute I=12MR2I = \frac{1}{2} M R^2 and α=2a\alpha = 2a

    τ=12MR2⋅2a=aMR2\tau = \frac{1}{2} M R^2 \cdot 2a = a M R^2

  6. Power (PP): Substitute τ=aMR2\tau = a M R^2 and ω=2at+b\omega = 2at + b into the power formula:

    P=τ⋅ω=(aMR2)⋅(2at+b)P = \tau \cdot \omega = (a M R^2) \cdot (2at + b), P=aMR2(2at+b)P = a M R^2 (2at + b)
Final Answer:

The power derived to the disc as a function of time is:

aMR2(2at+b)\boxed{a M R^2 (2at + b)}​

Moment of inertia about a diameter:

For a circular ring, the moment of inertia about an axis passing through its diameter (in its plane) is:

Opposite to the direction of r→

Explanation

Given position vector of particle in circular motion.
r→ = acosωti^ + asinωtj^
Velocity is time derivative of position.
v→ = dr→ dt = −aωsinωti^ + aωcosωtj^
Acceleration is time derivative of velocity.
a→ = dv→ dt = −aω2cosωti^ − aω2sinωtj^
Force is proportional to acceleration.
F→ = m a→
Acceleration vector is opposite in direction to position vector.
Therefore acceleration is antiparallel to position vector.

Q45

A thin circular disc of mass \(M\) and radius \(R\) is rotating in a horizontal plane about an axis passing through its centre and perpendicular to its plane with angular velocity \(\omega\). If another disc of same dimensions but of mass \(M / 2\) is placed gently on the first disc co-axially, then the new angular velocity of the system is :

[JEE Main 2024, 08 Apr (Shift 2)]

a

\(\frac{5}{4} \omega\)

b

\(\frac{3}{2} \omega\)

c

\(\frac{2}{3} \omega\)

d

\(\frac{4}{5} \omega\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

\(\frac{2}{3} \omega\)

Explanation

Since the second disc is placed gently, the net external torque on the system is zero.

Therefore, the angular momentum is conserved:

\[L_i = L_f\] \[I_1 \omega_1 = I_2 \omega_2\]

Initial Moment of Inertia: I1=12MR2

Final Moment of Inertia: I2=12MR2+12M2R2=34MR2

Equating the initial and final states:

\[\left(\frac{1}{2}MR^2\right)\omega = \left(\frac{3}{4}MR^2\right)\omega_2\] \[\frac{1}{2}\omega = \frac{3}{4}\omega_2\] \[\omega_2 = \frac{2}{3}\omega\]

Correct Option: C
