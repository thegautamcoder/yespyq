Q2

A solenoid of radius 10 cm carrying current 0.29 A and having total 200 turns. If magnetic field inside solenoid is\( 2.9 \times 10^{-4} \mathrm{~T}\). Find length of solenoid.(Shift - II Memory Based)

a

\(6 \pi \mathrm{~cm}\)

b

\(8 \pi \mathrm{~cm}\)

c

4.5 cm

d

16 cm

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

\(8 \pi \mathrm{~cm}\)

Explanation

We are given:

  • Radius of solenoid = r=10r = 10cm (not needed in calculations)
  • Current = I=0.29I = 0.29 A
  • Total number of turns = N=200N = 200
  • Magnetic field inside the solenoid = B=2.9×10−4B = 2.9 \times 10^{-4}

We need to determine the length of the solenoid (LL).

Step 1: Using the Magnetic Field Formula for a Solenoid

The magnetic field inside a solenoid is given by:

B=μ0nIB = \mu_0 n I

where:

  • μ0\mu_0​ is the permeability of free space, μ0=4π×10−7\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}

  • nnn is the number of turns per unit length, given by:

    n=NLn = \frac{N}{L}​

  • II is the current.

Rewriting the equation:

B=μ0NLIB = \mu_0 \frac{N}{L} I

Solving for LL:

L=μ0NIBL = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{B}​

Step 2: Substituting Values

L=(4π×10−7)(200)(0.29)2.9×10−4L = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) (200) (0.29)}{2.9 \times 10^{-4}}

​ L=(4π×10−7×200×0.29)2.9×10−4L = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7} \times 200 \times 0.29)}{2.9 \times 10^{-4}}

L=8π cm

Final Answer:

B8π cm\boxed{B} \quad 8\pi \text{ cm}

Q7

A square loop of side 2 cm is placed in a time varying magnetic field with magnitude as B = 0.4 sin (300t) Tesla. The normal to the plane of loop makes an angle of 60° with the field. The maximum induced emf produced in the loop is ______ mV.

[JEE Main 2026, 6 Apr (Shift 2)]

a

12

b

18

c

21

d

24

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

24

Explanation

Given Data

Side of the square loop (a) = 2 cm = 2×10-2 m

Area of the loop (\(A\)) = a2=(2×10-2)2=4×10-4 m2

Magnetic field (\(B\)) = 0.4sin(300t) T

Angle between the normal to the loop and the magnetic field (θ) = 60°

Concept & Formula

According to Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction, the induced emf (\(e\)) is the negative rate of change of magnetic flux (Φ):

\[e = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt}\]

Where magnetic flux is given by:

\[\Phi = BA\cos(\theta)\]

Calculation

First, substitute the given values into the flux equation:

\[\Phi = (0.4 \sin(300t)) \times (4 \times 10^{-4}) \times \cos(60^\circ)\] \[\Phi = 0.4 \times (4 \times 10^{-4}) \times 0.5 \times \sin(300t)\] \[\Phi = 0.8 \times 10^{-4} \sin(300t)\text{ Wb}\]

Next, differentiate the flux with respect to time (\(t\)) to find the induced emf:

\[e = -\frac{d}{dt} [0.8 \times 10^{-4} \sin(300t)]\] \[e = -0.8 \times 10^{-4} \times 300 \cos(300t)\] \[e = -240 \times 10^{-4} \cos(300t)\] \[e = -24 \times 10^{-3} \cos(300t)\text{ V}\]

The maximum induced emf (emax) occurs when cos(300t)=1:

\[e_{max} = 24 \times 10^{-3}\text{ V}\] \[e_{max} = 24\text{ mV}\]

Final Answer: 24 mV

Correct Option: D

Q10

A disc is rotating with an angular velocity \(\omega=10 \pi \mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}\) in a uniform magnetic field of \(B=0.4 \mathrm{~T}\), which is directed perpendicular to the plane of the disc. The radius of the disc is 20 cm . Find the potential difference between the centre and the rim of the disc.
(Take \(\pi=3.14\) )

(Shift - II Memory Based)

a

0.25 V

b

0.5 V

c

1 V

d

2 V

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

0.25 V

Explanation

The given problem involves a rotating disc in the presence of a uniform magnetic field, requiring us to determine the potential difference between the center and the rim.

Step 1: Understanding the Given Data

  • Angular velocity: ω=10π\omega = 10\pi

  • Magnetic field: B=0.4B = 0.4 (pointing perpendicular to the plane)

  • Radius of the disc: R=20R = 20

  • Formula to be used: The induced potential difference (emf) between the center and the rim of a rotating disc in a magnetic field is given by:

    V=12BωR2V = \frac{1}{2} B \omega R^2
Step 2: Substituting the Given Values

V=12×(0.4)×(10π)×(0.2)2V = \frac{1}{2} \times (0.4) \times (10\pi) \times (0.2)^2

Step 3: Simplifying the Expression

V=12×0.4×10π×0.04V = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.4 \times 10\pi \times 0.04

V=12×0.16πV = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.16\pi

V=0.08πV = 0.08\pi

Step 4: Calculating the Final Value

Using π≈3.14\pi \approx 3.14

V=0.08×3.14V = 0.08 \times 3.14

V≈0.251 VV \approx 0.251 \text{ V}

Final Answer:

The potential difference between the center and the rim of the disc is approximately 0.251 V.
