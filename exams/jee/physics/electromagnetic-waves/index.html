The relationship between the electric field (E0E_0) and magnetic field (B0B_0) in electromagnetic waves is given by:

Explanation

1. Direction: The wave propagates along the \(+z\)-axis (from \(t - z/c\)). The electric field \(\vec{E}\) is along the \(+x\)-axis (\(\hat{i}\)).

Combining these, \(\vec{B}=\hat{j}\frac{40}{c}\cos\omega(t-z/c)\).

d

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

ExplanationStep 1: Understanding Electromagnetic Wave Propagation

  • An electromagnetic wave (EM wave) consists of:

    • An electric field (EE)
    • A magnetic field (BB)
    • A wave propagation direction

  • These three quantities are mutually perpendicular following the right-hand rule:

    • If the wave propagates along +X-direction, then:
      • The electric field (EE) is along the Y-axis.
      • The magnetic field (BB) is along the Z-axis.
Step 2: Identifying the Correct Answer
  • Given that the wave propagates in the +X direction:
    • EE is along Y-axis.
    • BB is along Z-axis.

Correct answer:

B(Y,Z)\boxed{B} \quad (Y, Z)

Q25

For an electromagnetic wave propagating through vacuum, k→, E→ and ω represent propagation vector, electric field and angular frequency, respectively. The magnetic field associated with this wave is represented by:

[JEE Main 2026, 6 Apr (Shift 2)]

a

E→×k→ω

b

k→×E→ω

c

ω(E→×k→)

d

ω(k→×E→)

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

k→×E→ω

Explanation

From Maxwell's equations, specifically Faraday's Law of Induction, we have:

\[\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}\]

For a plane electromagnetic wave, the spatial derivative operator \(\nabla\) is replaced by ik→, and the time derivative operator ∂∂t is replaced by -iω.

Substituting these into the equation gives:

\[i\vec{k} \times \vec{E} = -(-i\omega)\vec{B}\]

Simplifying the equation:

\[i(\vec{k} \times \vec{E}) = i\omega \vec{B}\]

Canceling \(i\) from both sides and solving for the magnetic field B→, we get:

\[\vec{B} = \frac{\vec{k} \times \vec{E}}{\omega}\]

Therefore, the correct option is B.
