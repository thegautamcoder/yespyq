Q1

A car moving with a speed of 54 km / h takes a turn of radius 20 m . A simple pendulum is suspended from the ceiling of the car. Determine the angle made by the string of the pendulum with the vertical during the turning. (Take g=10m/s2 )

[JEE Main 2026, 8 Apr (Shift 2)]

a

tan-1(0.5)

b

tan-1(0.75)

c

tan-1(1.125)

d

tan-1(0.25)

tan-1(1.125)

Explanation

For the pendulum bob in the turning car,

tanθ=v2rg

Given:

v=54×518=15m/s

So,

tanθ=15220×10=1.125

Q2

A train is moving with a speed of \(12 m / s\) on rails which are \(1.5 m\) apart. To negotiate a curve of radius \(400 m\), the height by which the outer rail should be raised with respect to the inner rail is: (Given, \(g =10 m / s ^2\)):

a

5.4 cm

b

4.2 cm

c

6.0 cm

d

4.8 cm

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

5.4 cm

Explanation

Banking angle formula:

tanθ=v2rg

From geometry (small angles:)

tanθ≈sinθ=hl

Equating the two expressions:

hl=v2rg⇒h=v2lrg

Substituting the values:

h=(12)2×1.5400×10

h=144×1.54000

h=2164000=0.054 m

Convert to cm:

h=0.054×100=5.4 cm

Q4

An object is performing vertical circular motion. If its velocity at the topmost point is given by:

vtop=ngl,n>1v_{\text{top}} = n \sqrt{g l}, \quad n > 1

Determine the ratio of the kinetic energy at the bottom to that at the top.​

a

n2+4n2\frac{n^2+4}{n^2}​

b

n2n2+4\frac{n^2}{n^2 + 4}​

c

n2+2n2\frac{n^2+2}{n^2}​

d

n2n2+2\frac{n^2}{n^2+2}​

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

n2+4n2\frac{n^2+4}{n^2}​

ExplanationStep 1: Understanding the Motion

In a vertical circular motion, energy conservation is used to relate the velocities at the topmost and bottommost points.

  • Given velocity at the topmost point:

    vtop=ngl,n>1v_{\text{top}} = n \sqrt{g l}, \quad n > 1

  • Let velocity at the bottommost point be vbottomv_{\text{bottom}}

  • Applying energy conservation between the top and bottom points:

    Total Energy at the Top = Total Energy at the Bottom
Step 2: Applying Energy Conservation

  • Total energy at the topmost point:

    Etop=KEtop+PEtopE_{\text{top}} = KE_{\text{top}} + PE_{\text{top}} =12mvtop2+mg(2l)= \frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{top}}^2 + mg(2l)

    Since vtop=nglv_{\text{top}} = n \sqrt{g l}​we substitute:

    KEtop=12m(n2gl)=12mn2glKE_{\text{top}} = \frac{1}{2} m (n^2 g l) = \frac{1}{2} m n^2 g l
  • Etop=12mn2gl+2mglE_{\text{top}} = \frac{1}{2} m n^2 g l + 2 m g l

  • Total energy at the bottommost point:

    Ebottom=KEbottom+PEbottomE_{\text{bottom}} = KE_{\text{bottom}} + PE_{\text{bottom}}​

    Since at the bottommost point, PE = 0, we get:

    Ebottom=KEbottom=12mvbottom2E_{\text{bottom}} = KE_{\text{bottom}} = \frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{bottom}}^2​

  • By energy conservation:

    Ebottom=EtopE_{\text{bottom}} = E_{\text{top}}
  • 12mvbottom2=12mn2gl+2mgl\frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{bottom}}^2 = \frac{1}{2} m n^2 g l + 2 m g l

    Canceling mmm and multiplying by 2:

    vbottom2=n2gl+4glv_{\text{bottom}}^2 = n^2 g l + 4 g l
  • vbottom2=(n2+4)glv_{\text{bottom}}^2 = (n^2 + 4) g l
  • vbottom=(n2+4)glv_{\text{bottom}} = \sqrt{(n^2 + 4) g l}
Step 3: Finding the Ratio of KE (Bottom/Top)

KEbottomKEtop=12mvbottom212mvtop2\frac{KE_{\text{bottom}}}{KE_{\text{top}}} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{bottom}}^2}{\frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{top}}^2}​​

Canceling 12m\frac{1}{2} m:

KEbottomKEtop=(n2+4)gln2gl\frac{KE_{\text{bottom}}}{KE_{\text{top}}} = \frac{(n^2 + 4) g l}{n^2 g l} =n2+4n2= \frac{n^2 + 4}{n^2}​

Comparing with the options, the correct answer is:

A.n2+4n2​​
