Consider following statements:
A. Surface tension arises due to extra energy of the molecules at the interior as compared to the molecules at the surface, of a liquid.

D. The onset of turbulence is determined by Reynold's number.
E. In a steady flow two stream lines never intersect.

Explanation

Surface tension is property of surface molecules not interior molecules. In case of liquid as temperature increases surface tension decreases. In case of gases due to increase in temperature collision will be more frequent hence viscosity increases.

Q7

A sphere of mass MM is dropped into glycerin. The density of glycerin is half the density of the sphere. After some time, the sphere moves down with constant velocity. What is the viscous force acting on the sphere?

(Shift II Memory Based)​

a

Mg2\frac{Mg}{2}​

b

Mg3\frac{Mg}{3}​

c

Mg4\frac{Mg}{4}​

d

​Mg6\frac{Mg}{6}​

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

Mg2\frac{Mg}{2}​

ExplanationKey Concept:

At terminal velocity (constant velocity), the net force acting on the sphere is zero. The forces acting on the sphere are:

  1. Gravitational Force (FgF_g): Downward force due to the weight of the sphere. Fg=MgF_g = Mg
  2. Buoyant Force (FbF_b​): Upward force due to the displaced glycerin. Fb=ρglycerinVgF_b = \rho_{\text{glycerin}} V g where ρglycerin\rho_{\text{glycerin}}is the density of glycerin, VV is the volume of the sphere, and gg is the acceleration due to gravity.
  3. Viscous Force (FvF_v​): Upward force due to the viscosity of glycerin. This balances the remaining downward force at terminal velocity.

From equilibrium at terminal velocity:

Fv=Fg−FbF_v = F_g - F_b​

Step 1: Relationship Between Densities

The density of glycerin (ρglycerin\rho_{\text{glycerin}}) is half the density of the sphere (ρsphere\rho_{\text{sphere}}):

ρglycerin=ρsphere2\rho_{\text{glycerin}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{sphere}}}{2}​​

The mass MM of the sphere is related to its density and volume:

M=ρsphereVM = \rho_{\text{sphere}} V

Substitute ρglycerin=ρsphere2\rho_{\text{glycerin}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{sphere}}}{2}​​ into FbF_b:

Fb=ρsphere2Vg=M2gF_b = \frac{\rho_{\text{sphere}}}{2} V g = \frac{M}{2} g

Step 2: Calculate the Viscous Force

Using Fv=Fg−FbF_v = F_g - F_b:

Fv=Mg−M2gF_v = Mg - \frac{M}{2}g

Simplify:

Fv=M2gF_v = \frac{M}{2} g

Final Answer:

The viscous force acting on the sphere is:

Mg2\boxed{\frac{Mg}{2}}​​

(Shift - II Memory Based)​

a

b

VA−VB=2(PA−PB)ρV_A - V_B = \frac{2(P_A - P_B)}{\rho}​

c

​VA2−VB2=2(PB−PA)ρV_A^2 - V_B^2 = \frac{2(P_B - P_A)}{\rho}​

Explanation

This question is based on Bernoulli's equation, which states:

P+12ρV2+ρgh=constantP + \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 + \rho g h = \text{constant}

Since the pipe is horizontal (hhh is constant), the equation simplifies to:

P+12ρV2=constantP + \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 = \text{constant}

For points A and B:

PA+12ρVA2=PB+12ρVB2P_A + \frac{1}{2} \rho V_A^2 = P_B + \frac{1}{2} \rho V_B^2​

Rearranging:

12ρVA2−12ρVB2=PB−PA, ​ ρ2(VA2−VB2)=PB−PA​

Divide through by ρ/2\rho/2:

VA2−VB2=2(PB−PA)ρ​ Final Relation:

The correct option is:

VA2−VB2=2(PB−PA)ρ

Q11

A small spherical ball of radius \(r\), falling through a viscous medium of negligible density has terminal velocity ' \(v\) '. Another ball of the same mass but of radius \(2 r\), falling through the same viscous medium will have terminal velocity:

[JEE Main 2024, 31 Jan (Shift 2)]

a

\(4 \mathrm{~v}\)

b

\(2 \mathrm{~v}\)

c

\(\frac{\mathrm{v}}{2}\)

d

\(\frac{v}{4}\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

\(\frac{\mathrm{v}}{2}\)

Explanation

Terminal velocity of the ball through a viscous medium

\(v=\frac{2}{9} \times \frac{g}{\eta}(\rho-\sigma) r^2\)


Or \(v=\frac{2}{9} \times \frac{g}{\eta}(\rho) r^2\)
Because for viscous medium of negligible density

$$
\begin{aligned}
& (\sigma=0) \therefore \\
& v=\frac{2}{9} \times \frac{g}{\eta} \times \frac{m}{\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3} \times r^2 \\
& {\left[\because \rho=\frac{m}{\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3}\right]}
\end{aligned}
$$


Or \(v=\frac{2}{9} \times \frac{g}{\eta} \times \frac{m}{\frac{4}{3} \pi r}\)

\(\Rightarrow v \propto \frac{1}{r}\)


For the second ball \(v \propto \frac{1}{2 r}\)

\(\therefore \frac{v}{v}=\frac{2 r}{r} \text { Or } v=\frac{v}{2}\)
