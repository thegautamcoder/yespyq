✓ Correct answer: b)

15 cm and 17 cm

Explanation

To solve this problem, we need to use the lens maker's formula, which relates the power of a lens to its radii of curvature and the refractive index of the material. The lens maker's formula is given by:

\(\frac{1}{f}=(n-1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1}-\frac{1}{R_2}\right)\)

where:
- \(n\) is the refractive index of the lens material
- \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) are the radii of curvature of the lens surfaces
- \(f\) is the focal length of the lens

For a bi-convex lens with equal radii of curvature, \(R_1=R_2=R\), the formula modifies to:

\(\frac{1}{f}=(n-1)\left(\frac{1}{R}-\frac{1}{-R}\right)=\frac{2(n-1)}{R}\)


Given that \(R=\frac{1}{6} \mathrm{~cm}\), the power \(P\) of the original lens can be expressed as:

\(P=\frac{1}{f}=\frac{2(n-1)}{\frac{1}{6}}=12(n-1)\)


Now, if we replace it with another convex lens having different radii of curvature \(R_1\) and \(R_2\), keeping the power the same:

\(\frac{1}{f}=(n-1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1}-\frac{1}{R_2}\right)\)


Substituting the known power:

\(12(n-1)=(n-1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1}-\frac{1}{R_2}\right)\)


By cancelling \((n-1)\) from both sides (assuming \(n \neq 1\) ):

\(12=\frac{1}{R_1}-\frac{1}{R_2}\)


Checking the options:
- \(R_1=\frac{1}{5} \mathrm{~cm}, R_2=\frac{1}{7} \mathrm{~cm}\)
