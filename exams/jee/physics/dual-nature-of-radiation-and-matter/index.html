Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

Explanation

The energy of the incident photon (EE) is related to the work function and the stopping potential by:

E=ϕ+eVE = \phi + eVE

Here:

  • ϕ=3.4 eV\phi = 3.4 \, \text{eV}
  • eV=1.6 eVeV = 1.6 \, \text{eV}
  • The total energy of the incident photon:

E=3.4+1.6=5.0 eVE = 3.4 + 1.6 = 5.0 \, \text{eV}

The energy of the photon is also given by:

E=hcλE = \frac{h c}{\lambda}​

Rearranging for λ\lambda:

λ=hcE\lambda = \frac{h c}{E}​

Substitute the given values:

λ=124005=2480 A˚\lambda = \frac{12400}{5} = 2480 \, \text{\AA}

Convert to nanometers (since 1 A˚=0.1 nm1 \, \text{\AA} = 0.1 \, \text{nm}1A˚=0.1nm):

λ=248 nm\lambda = 248 \, \text{nm}λ=248nm

Correct option (b) 248nm

Q9

Monochromatic light of frequency \(6 \times 10^{14} Hz\) is produced by a laser. The power emitted is \(2 \times 10^{-3} W\). How many photons per second on an average, are emitted by the source?
(Given \(h =6.63 \times 10^{-34} Js\) )

[JEE Main 2024, 01 Feb (Shift 2)]

a

\(6 \times 10^{15}\)

b

\(5 \times 10^{15}\)

c

\(7 \times 10^{16}\)

d

\(9 \times 10^{18}\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

\(5 \times 10^{15}\)

Explanation

Photons are the packets of energy. Power emitted,
\(P=2 \times 10^{-3} W\)

Energy of photon,
$$
\begin{aligned}
& E=h v \\
& =6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 10^{14} J
\end{aligned}
$$
\(h\) being Planck's constant.
Number of photons emitted per second
$$
\begin{aligned}
& n=\frac{P}{E} \\
& =\frac{2 \times 10^{-3}}{6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 6 \times 10^{14}} \\
& =5 \times 10^{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

Q13

A photo-emissive substance is illuminated with a radiation of wavelength λi so that it releases electrons with de-Broglie wavelength λe. The longest wavelength of radiation that can emit photoelectron is λ0. Expression for de-Broglie wavelength is given by :
( m : mass of the electron, h : Planck's constant and c : speed of light)

a

λe=h2mc1λi-1λ0

b

λe=hλ02mc

c

λe=h2mc1λi-1λ0

d

λe=hλi2mc

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

λe=h2mc1λi-1λ0

Explanation

For photoemission,
\[
hf_i=\phi+K_{\max}, \qquad \phi=\frac{hc}{\lambda_0}, \qquad f_i=\frac{c}{\lambda_i}.
\]
Hence the kinetic energy of emitted electrons is
\[
K_{\max}=hc\!\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_i}-\frac{1}{\lambda_0}\right).
\]

Using de Broglie and non-relativistic relations,
\[
K_{\max}=\frac{p^2}{2m}, \qquad \lambda_e=\frac{h}{p}.
\]
Therefore
\[
p=\sqrt{2mK_{\max}}
=\sqrt{2mhc\!\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_i}-\frac{1}{\lambda_0}\right)},
\]
and
\[
\boxed{\;\lambda_e
=\frac{h}{p}
=\sqrt{\frac{h}{2mc\!\left(\dfrac{1}{\lambda_i}-\dfrac{1}{\lambda_0}\right)}}\;}
\]
