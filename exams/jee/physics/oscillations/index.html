a

\(
\frac{8}{9} m

\(

✓ Correct answer: b)

\)

Explanation

At altitude \(h=2R\) above Earth’s surface, the distance from Earth’s center is \(r=R+h=3R\).
Acceleration due to gravity there is
\[
g' = g\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^2 = g\left(\frac{R}{3R}\right)^2 = \frac{g}{9}.
\]

For a seconds pendulum \(T=2\,\text{s}\) and
\[
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g'}} \;\Rightarrow\; L=\frac{g' T^2}{4\pi^2}.
\]
With \(g=\pi^2\,\text{m s}^{-2}\),
\[
L=\frac{\left(\dfrac{g}{9}\right)\, (2)^2}{4\pi^2}
=\frac{g}{9}\cdot\frac{4}{4\pi^2}
=\frac{g}{9\pi^2}
=\frac{\pi^2}{9\pi^2}
=\frac{1}{9}\ \text{m}.
\]

\[
\boxed{\dfrac{1}{9}\ \text{m}}
\]

Q7

Two particles of same mass are performing SHM vertically with two different springs of spring constants \(K_1\) and \(K_2\). If amplitude of both is same. Find ratio of the maximum speed of two particles.

a

\(\sqrt{\frac{K_1}{K_2}}\)

b

\(\sqrt{K_1 K_2}\)

c

\(\sqrt{\frac{K_2}{K_1}}\)

d

\(\sqrt{\frac{K_1+K_2}{K_1-K_2}}\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

\(\sqrt{\frac{K_1}{K_2}}\)

ExplanationStep 1: Understanding SHM and Maximum Speed

For a simple harmonic motion (SHM), the maximum speed Vmax​ of a particle is given by:

Vmax=Aω

where:

  • AA is the amplitude of oscillation.

  • ω\omega is the angular frequency, given by:

    ω=km\omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}​

Thus, the maximum speed becomes:

Vmax=Akm​

Step 2: Finding the Ratio of Maximum Speeds

Let the two particles have spring constants K1K_1​ and K2K_2 but the same mass mm and same amplitude AA.

For the first particle:

vmax⁡1=AK1mv_{\max1} = A \sqrt{\frac{K_1}{m}}​

For the second particle:

vmax⁡2=AK2mv_{\max2} = A \sqrt{\frac{K_2}{m}}

Taking the ratio:

vmax⁡1vmax⁡2=AK1mAK2m\frac{v_{\max1}}{v_{\max2}} = \frac{A \sqrt{\frac{K_1}{m}}}{A \sqrt{\frac{K_2}{m}}}​​

Canceling AAA and simplifying:

vmax⁡1vmax⁡2=K1K2\frac{v_{\max1}}{v_{\max2}} = \sqrt{\frac{K_1}{K_2}}

Step 3: Selecting the Correct Option

AK1K2\boxed{A} \quad \sqrt{\frac{K_1}{K_2}}

A spring stretches by 2 mm when it is loaded with a mass of 200 g. From equilibrium position the mass is further pulled down by 2 mm and released. The frequency associated with the system and maximum energy in the spring are _____ Hz and ________ J, respectively.

[JEE Main 2026, 6 Apr (Shift 2)]

550π and 8×10-3

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

550π and 8×10-3

Explanation

Given Data

Mass m=200 g=0.2 kg

Equilibrium extension x0=2 mm=2×10-3 m

Amplitude (further pull) A=2 mm=2×10-3 m

Find the spring constant (\(k\))

At the initial equilibrium position, the spring force balances the weight:

\[mg = kx_0\] \[k = \frac{0.2 \times 10}{2 \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{2}{2 \times 10^{-3}} = 1000\text{ N/m}\]

Calculate the frequency (\(f\))

Using the standard formula for the frequency of a spring-mass system:

\[f = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}\] \[f = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{\frac{1000}{0.2}} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \sqrt{5000}\] \[f = \frac{10\sqrt{50}}{2\pi} = \frac{5\sqrt{50}}{\pi}\text{ Hz}\]

Calculate the maximum energy in the spring

Key Concept: The maximum energy stored in the spring occurs at the lowest point of oscillation, where the total extension is the sum of the equilibrium stretch and the amplitude (xmax=x0+A).

\[x_{max} = 2\text{ mm} + 2\text{ mm} = 4\text{ mm} = 4 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}\]

Now, find the maximum elastic potential energy:

\[E_{max} = \frac{1}{2}kx_{max}^2\] \[E_{max} = \frac{1}{2} \times 1000 \times (4 \times 10^{-3})^2\] \[E_{max} = 500 \times 16 \times 10^{-6}\] \[E_{max} = 8000 \times 10^{-6} = 8 \times 10^{-3}\text{ J}\]

Correct Option: A

Q13

A particle is executing simple harmonic motion. Its amplitude is \(A\) and time period is 5 sec. The time required by it to move from x=A to x=A2 is ______sec.

[JEE Main 2026, 6 Apr (Shift 1)]

a

14

b

54

c

58

d

38

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

58

Explanation

Since the particle is moving from the extreme position (\(x = A\)), we use the cosine equation for simple harmonic motion:

\[x = A \cos(\omega t)\]

Substitute the target position x=A2:

\[\frac{A}{\sqrt{2}} = A \cos(\omega t)\] \[\cos(\omega t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\]

This gives the phase angle:

\[\omega t = \frac{\pi}{4}\]

Substitute angular frequency ω=2πT:

\[\frac{2\pi}{T} \cdot t = \frac{\pi}{4}\]

Solving for \(t\):

\[t = \frac{T}{8}\]

Given the time period \(T = 5\) sec:

\[t = \frac{5}{8} \text{ sec}\]

  1. Key concepts:
