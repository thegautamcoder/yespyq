In Bohr's model, radius of \( n^\text{th} \) orbit in hydrogen-like atoms is given by:

\[

Q5

Bohr's model is applicable for single electron atom of atomic number \(Z\). Dependency of frequency of rotation of electron in \(n^{\text {th }}\) principal quantum number is proportional to

a

\(Z / n^2\)

b

\(Z^2 / n^3\)

c

\(n^3 / Z\)

d

\(Z / n\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

\(Z^2 / n^3\)

ExplanationStep 1: Understanding Bohr’s Model

  • In Bohr’s atomic model, an electron revolves in a circular orbit with quantized angular momentum.

  • The frequency of revolution (ff) is given by:

    f=v2πrf = \frac{v}{2\pi r}​

    where:

    • vv is the electron's speed in orbit,
    • rr is the radius of the orbit.
Step 2: Expressions for Velocity and Radius in Bohr’s Model

  • Velocity of electron in the nnn-th orbit:

    vn∝Znv_n \propto \frac{Z}{n}​

  • Radius of the orbit:

    rn∝n2Zr_n \propto \frac{n^2}{Z}​
Step 3: Finding the Frequency Dependency

Using f=v2πrf = \frac{v}{2\pi r}

fn∝Znn2Zf_n \propto \frac{\frac{Z}{n}}{\frac{n^2}{Z}}

fn∝Z2n3f_n \propto \frac{Z^2}{n^3}​

Q12

Infrared light of wavelength 900 nm900 \, \text{nm} is used for muscle pain relief. Which of the following transitions in the hydrogen atom can produce this wavelength?

(shift 1 Memory based)

a

Lyman: 3→13 \to 1

b

Balmer: 5→25 \to 2

c

Paschen: 5→35 \to 3

d

Paschen: ∞→3\infty \to 3

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

Paschen: ∞→3\infty \to 3

Explanation

The wavelength of light emitted during an electronic transition in the hydrogen atom is determined using the Rydberg formula:

1λ=R(1n12−1n22),\frac{1}{\lambda} = R \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right),

where:

  • R=1.097×107 m−1R = 1.097 \times 10^7 \, \text{m}^{-1} (Rydberg constant),
  • n1n_1​ and n2n_2 are the principal quantum numbers (n2>n1n_2 > n_1​).
Step 1: Identify the Series:
  • Lyman Series: Transitions to n1=1n_1 = 1 (UV region).
  • Balmer Series: Transitions to n1=2n_1 = 2 (visible region).
  • Paschen Series: Transitions to n1=3n_1 = 3 (infrared region).

Since 900 nm900 \, \text{nm} falls in the infrared region, the transition must belong to the Paschen series.

Step 2: Verify Wavelength:

For n1=3n_1 = 3 (Paschen series) and n2=∞n_2 = \infty:

1λ=R(132−1∞2),\frac{1}{\lambda} = R \left( \frac{1}{3^2} - \frac{1}{\infty^2} \right),

1λ=R(19−0),\frac{1}{\lambda} = R \left( \frac{1}{9} - 0 \right),

λ=1R⋅19=9R.\lambda = \frac{1}{R \cdot \frac{1}{9}} = \frac{9}{R}.λ

Substitute R=1.097×107 m−1R = 1.097 \times 10^7 \, \text{m}^{-1}

λ=91.097×107=8.2×10−7 m=820 nm.\lambda = \frac{9}{1.097 \times 10^7} = 8.2 \times 10^{-7} \, \text{m} = 820 \, \text{nm}.

The transition n2=∞→n1=3n_2 = \infty \to n_1 = 3 in the Paschen series matches the infrared wavelength range close to 900 nm900 \, \text{nm}.

Correct Answer: (d) Paschen: ∞→3\infty \to 3

Q16

Statement 1: Graph of frequency f of X ray and atomic number Z of heavy nucleus is not straight line, in X ray emission.
Statement 2: Graph of square root of frequency f of X ray and atomic number Z of heavy nucleus is straight line, in X ray emission.

(Shift - II Memory Based)

a

Statement 1 is correct and statement 2 is correct

b

Statement 1 is incorrect and statement 2 is correct

c

Statement 1 is correct and statement 2 is incorrect

d

Statement 1 is incorrect and statement 2 is incorrect

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

Statement 1 is correct and statement 2 is correct

Explanation

Moseley's law states that f=kz-b, where k is a constant and b is the screening constant. This implies that the square root of frequency is directly proportional to Z, resulting in a straight-line graph. However, f itself does not follow a linear relationship with Z. Moseley's law is primarily valid for heavy elements, and deviations occur for light atoms due to weaker screening effects and other relativistic considerations. Hence, both statements are correct.

Q17

The ratio of the shortest wavelength of Balmer series to the shortest wavelength of Lyman series for hydrogen atom is :

[JEE Main 2021, 25 July (Shift 2)]

a

1:2

b

4:1

c

3:5

d

1:4

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

4:1

Explanation

Shortest wavelength in a spectral series corresponds to transition from \( n = \infty \) to the series limit level.

- For Lyman series, lower level is \( n = 1 \)
\[
\frac{1}{\lambda_L} = R \left( \frac{1}{1^2} - \frac{1}{\infty^2} \right) = R
\Rightarrow \lambda_L = \frac{1}{R}
\]

- For Balmer series, lower level is \( n = 2 \)
\[
\frac{1}{\lambda_B} = R \left( \frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{\infty^2} \right) = R \cdot \frac{1}{4}
\Rightarrow \lambda_B = \frac{4}{R}
\]

Now, taking the ratio:

\[
\frac{\lambda_B}{\lambda_L} = \frac{4/R}{1/R} = 4 : 1
\]

Q19

An electron is moving in a magnetic field BB in a circular orbit. Assume Bohr's quantization to be valid. Find the radius of the orbit in the 1st excited state.

a

4hπBe\sqrt{\frac{4h}{\pi B e}}

b

h2πBe\sqrt{\frac{h}{2 \pi B e}}

c

hπBe\sqrt{\frac{h}{\pi B e}}

d

2hπBe\sqrt{\frac{2h}{\pi B e}}

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

hπBe\sqrt{\frac{h}{\pi B e}}

ExplanationKey Concepts:

  1. Bohr’s Quantization Rule: The angular momentum of the electron is quantized:

    mvr=nh2πm v r = n \frac{h}{2\pi}​

    where:

    • mm is the mass of the electron,
    • vv is its velocity,
    • rr is the radius of the orbit,
    • nn is the quantum number (n=2n = 2 for the 1st excited state),
    • hh is Planck's constant.

  2. Magnetic Force Provides Centripetal Force: The force on a moving charge in a magnetic field is:

    Fmagnetic=evBF_{\text{magnetic}} = e v B

    This provides the centripetal force:

    Fmagnetic=mv2rF_{\text{magnetic}} = \frac{m v^2}{r}​
Step 1: Equating Forces

From the magnetic force equation:

evB=mv2re v B = \frac{m v^2}{r}​

Simplify to find vv:

v=eBrmv = \frac{e B r}{m}​ Step 2: Use Bohr's Quantization

Substitute v=eBrmv = \frac{e B r}{m}​ into Bohr’s quantization rule:

m⋅eBrm⋅r=nh2πm \cdot \frac{e B r}{m} \cdot r = n \frac{h}{2\pi}​

Simplify:

eBr2=nh2πe B r^2 = n \frac{h}{2\pi}​

Rearrange to solve for r2r^2:

r2=nh2πeBr^2 = \frac{n h}{2\pi e B}​

Thus:

r=nh2πeBr = \sqrt{\frac{n h}{2\pi e B}} Step 3: For the 1st Excited State

For the 1st excited state, n=2n = 2:

r=2h2πeB=hπeBr = \sqrt{\frac{2 h}{2\pi e B}} = \sqrt{\frac{h}{\pi e B}}​ Final Answer:

(C) hπBe\sqrt{\frac{h}{\pi Be}}
