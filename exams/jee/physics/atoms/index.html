For n1=3n_1 = 3 (Paschen series) and n2=∞n_2 = \infty:

Q19

An electron is moving in a magnetic field BB in a circular orbit. Assume Bohr's quantization to be valid. Find the radius of the orbit in the 1st excited state.

a

4hπBe\sqrt{\frac{4h}{\pi B e}}

b

h2πBe\sqrt{\frac{h}{2 \pi B e}}

c

hπBe\sqrt{\frac{h}{\pi B e}}

d

2hπBe\sqrt{\frac{2h}{\pi B e}}

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

hπBe\sqrt{\frac{h}{\pi B e}}

ExplanationKey Concepts:

  1. Bohr’s Quantization Rule: The angular momentum of the electron is quantized:

    mvr=nh2πm v r = n \frac{h}{2\pi}​

    where:

    • mm is the mass of the electron,
    • vv is its velocity,
    • rr is the radius of the orbit,
    • nn is the quantum number (n=2n = 2 for the 1st excited state),
    • hh is Planck's constant.

  2. Magnetic Force Provides Centripetal Force: The force on a moving charge in a magnetic field is:

    Fmagnetic=evBF_{\text{magnetic}} = e v B

    This provides the centripetal force:

    Fmagnetic=mv2rF_{\text{magnetic}} = \frac{m v^2}{r}​
Step 1: Equating Forces

From the magnetic force equation:

evB=mv2re v B = \frac{m v^2}{r}​

Simplify to find vv:

v=eBrmv = \frac{e B r}{m}​ Step 2: Use Bohr's Quantization

Substitute v=eBrmv = \frac{e B r}{m}​ into Bohr’s quantization rule:

m⋅eBrm⋅r=nh2πm \cdot \frac{e B r}{m} \cdot r = n \frac{h}{2\pi}​

Simplify:

eBr2=nh2πe B r^2 = n \frac{h}{2\pi}​

Rearrange to solve for r2r^2:

r2=nh2πeBr^2 = \frac{n h}{2\pi e B}​

Thus:

r=nh2πeBr = \sqrt{\frac{n h}{2\pi e B}} Step 3: For the 1st Excited State

For the 1st excited state, n=2n = 2:

r=2h2πeB=hπeBr = \sqrt{\frac{2 h}{2\pi e B}} = \sqrt{\frac{h}{\pi e B}}​ Final Answer:

(C) hπBe\sqrt{\frac{h}{\pi Be}}
