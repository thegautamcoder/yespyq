✓ Correct answer: d)

Explanation

N2O5(g)→2NO2(g)+12O2(g)

Let the initial pressure of N2O5 be P0.
Initial moles: Only N2O5 is present →n0 =P0 (as per ideal gas law).
Let x be the fraction of reaction completed.

Pfinal =PN2O5+PNO2+PO2
Pfinal =0.5P0+P0+0.25P0=1.75P0=74P0

Q12

The solubility of barium iodate in an aqueous solution prepared by mixing 200 mL of 0.010 M barium nitrate with 100 mL of 0.10 M sodium iodate is \(X \times 10^{-6} mol dm ^{-3}\). The value of \(X\) is________ .

Use: Solubility product constant ( \(K_{ sp }\) ) of barium iodate \(=1.58 \times 10^{-9}\)

[JEE Advanced 2025, Paper-2]

a

2

b

4

c

6

d

8

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

4

Explanation

Let the sparingly soluble salt be
Ba(IO₃)₂(s) ⇌ Ba²⁺(aq) + 2 IO₃⁻(aq)

Given: Ksp = 1.58 × 10⁻⁹

Step 1: Moles after mixing

  • Ba(NO₃)₂:
    Moles of Ba²⁺ = 0.010 mol L⁻¹ × 0.200 L = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol

  • NaIO₃:
    Moles of IO₃⁻ = 0.10 mol L⁻¹ × 0.100 L = 1.00 × 10⁻² mol

Total volume after mixing = 200 mL + 100 mL = 300 mL = 0.300 L

Initial concentrations in the mixed solution (before precipitation):
[Ba²⁺]₀ = (2.00 × 10⁻³) / 0.300 = 6.67 × 10⁻³ M
[IO₃⁻]₀ = (1.00 × 10⁻²) / 0.300 = 3.33 × 10⁻² M

To consume all Ba²⁺, moles of IO₃⁻ required = 2 × 2.00 × 10⁻³ = 4.00 × 10⁻³ mol

Hence IO₃⁻ in excess after (almost complete) precipitation:
= 1.00 × 10⁻² − 4.00 × 10⁻³ = 6.00 × 10⁻³ mol

So, in 0.300 L,
[IO₃⁻] ≈ (6.00 × 10⁻³) / 0.300 = 2.00 × 10⁻² M

Because Ksp is very small, the change in [IO₃⁻] due to the small solubility is negligible, so we take [IO₃⁻] ≈ 0.020 M at equilibrium.

Step 2: Use Ksp

Ksp = [Ba²⁺][IO₃⁻]²

Let the solubility of Ba(IO₃)₂ in this medium be S mol dm⁻³.
Then [Ba²⁺] = S and [IO₃⁻] ≈ 0.020 M, so

S × (0.020)² = 1.58 × 10⁻⁹

S = 1.58 × 10⁻⁹ / (4.0 × 10⁻⁴)
= (1.58 / 4.0) × 10⁻⁵
= 0.395 × 10⁻⁵
= 3.95 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³

Given S = X × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³,

X = 3.95

Q14

A weak acid HA has degree of dissociation x. Which option gives the correct expression of ( pH- pKa ?

a

log(1+2x)

b

0

c

logx1-x

d

log1-xx

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

logx1-x

Explanation

We know the dissociation equation for a weak acid HA :

Ka=H+A-[HA]

Since H+=Cx and A-=Cx, while [HA] =C(1-x), we get:

Ka=Cx21-x

Taking log on both sides:

logKa=logC+2logx-log(1-x)

Since pH=-logH+, we get:

pH=-logC-logx

Similarly, pKa=-logKα, so:

pKa=-logC-2logx+log(1-x)

Now, subtracting:

pH-pKa=(-logC-logx)-(-logC-2logx+log(1-x))=logx-log(1-x)=logx1-x

& A_2 B(g) \rightleftharpoons A_2(g)+\frac{1}{2} B_2(g) \\
& t=0 ~p_0 \\
& t=t_{\text {eq }} ~p_0(1-\alpha) \quad p_0 \alpha \quad p_0 \frac{\alpha}{2} \\
& P=p_0+p_0 \frac{\alpha}{2} \\
& P=p_0\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right) \quad\left(P \approx p_0\right)
\end{aligned}\)

\(\begin{aligned}
& \text { At equilibrium } K_p=\frac{\left(p_{A_2}\right)\left(p_{B_2}\right)}{\left(p_{A_2 B}\right)}=(\alpha \ll 1) \\
& k_p=\frac{\left(p_0 \alpha\right)\left(p_0 \frac{\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{p_0(1-\alpha)}=k_p=\alpha\left(p \frac{\alpha}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \\
& \frac{K_p}{p^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\alpha^{3 / 2}}{2^{1 / 2}} \\
& \frac{2 K_p^2}{P}=\alpha^3 \\
& \sqrt[3]{\frac{2 K_p^2}{P}}=\alpha
\end{aligned}\)
