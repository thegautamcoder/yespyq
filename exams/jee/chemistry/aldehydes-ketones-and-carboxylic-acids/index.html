Q2

An optically active alkyl halide C4H9Br[A] reacts with hot KOH dissolved in ethanol and forms alkene [B] as major product which reacts with bromine to give dibromide [C]. The compound [C] is converted into a gas [D] upon reacting with alcoholic NaNH2. During hydration 18 gram of water is added to 1 mole of gas [D] on warming with mercuric sulphate and dilute acid at 333 K to form compound [E]. The IUPAC name of compound [ E ] is

[JEE Main 2025, 2 Apr (Shift 1)]

a

But-2-yne

b

Butan-2-ol

c

Butan-2-one

d

Butan-1-al

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

Butan-2-one

Explanation

Optically active C4H9Br must be 2-bromobutane.

2-bromobutane + alcoholic KOH → but-2-ene (major)
but-2-ene + Br2 → 2,3-dibromobutane
2,3-dibromobutane + alcoholic NaNH2→ but-2-yne (gas)

Hydration of but-2-yne with HgSO4 and dilute acid gives a ketone.
Addition of 18 g water (1 mole) confirms ketone formation.

Final product is butan-2-one.

Q3

Both acetaldehyde and acetone (individually) undergo which of the following reactions?
A. Iodoform Reaction
B. Cannizzaro Reaction
C. Aldol Condensation
D. Tollen's Test
E. Clemmensen Reduction

Choose the correct answer from the options given below

a

A, B and D Only

b

A, C and E Only

c

B, C and D Only

d

C and E Only

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

A, C and E Only

Explanation

Cannizzaro Reaction:
- Cannizzaro reaction occurs in aldoses (aldehydes with no α-hydrogen).
- Acetaldehyde CH3CHO has an α-hydrogen, so it does NOT undergo the Cannizzaro reaction.
- Acetone CH3COCH3 is a ketone and also does not undergo the Cannizzaro reaction.
Tollen's Test:
- Tollen's test detects aldehydes (it oxidizes them to carboxylic acids).
- Acetaldehyde CH3CHO is an aldehyde, so it gives a positive Tollen's test.
- Acetone CH3COCH3 is a ketone, and ketones do not give Tollen's test.

Choose the correct option about maior product

Memory Based Question 28/Jan/25 Evening shift

'C' gives Fehling's solution test

b

'C' can be prepared by reacting PhMgBr with CO2

c

'C' gives Tollen's test

d

'C' can give effervescence with NaHCO3

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

'C' gives Tollen's test

Explanation

The given reaction sequence involves:

  1. C₆H₁₂ → A (Se/Δ): Dehydrogenation converts cyclohexane to benzene (A = C₆H₆).
  2. A → B (AlCl₃/CH₃Cl): Friedel-Crafts alkylation forms toluene (B = C₆H₅CH₃).
  3. B → C (CrO₂Cl₂/H₃O⁺): Etard oxidation converts toluene to benzaldehyde (C = C₆H₅CHO).

Checking options:

  • C does not give Fehling’s test (A).
  • C is not formed from PhMgBr + CO₂ (B).
  • C gives Tollen’s test due to the –CHO group (C ).
  • C does not react with NaHCO₃ (D).

Final answer: (C) ‘C’ gives Tollen’s test.

Answer & explanation — Premium

✓ Correct answer: a)

Propyne (CH₃-C≡CH) undergoes partial hydrogenation to form propene (CH₃-CH=CH₂).

Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

A, C & E only

Explanation

The Aldol Condensation involves the reaction of two aldehydes or ketones (with at least one alpha-hydrogen) in the presence of a base, leading to the formation of a β-hydroxy ketone or aldehyde, which may further undergo dehydration to give an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compound. Acetaldehyde can undergo aldol condensation because it has an alpha-hydrogen. Acetone can also undergo aldol condensation since it has alpha-hydrogens as well. Both acetaldehyde and acetone undergo aldol condensation. The Tollen's test detects aldehydes, which are oxidized to carboxylates while the Tollen’s reagent (silver ammonia complex) is reduced to metallic silver. Acetaldehyde can undergo Tollen's test because it is an aldehyde. Acetone cannot undergo Tollen’s test because it is a ketone and does not react with Tollen's reagent. Only acetaldehyde undergoes Tollen's test. The Clemmensen Reduction involves the reduction of ketones or aldehydes to alkanes using zinc amalgam in the presence of hydrochloric acid. Acetaldehyde can undergo Clemmensen reduction to form ethane. Acetone can also undergo Clemmensen reduction to form propane. Both acetaldehyde and acetone undergo Clemmensen reduction.
