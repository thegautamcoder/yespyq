Q8

During the detection of acidic radical present in a salt, a student gets a pale yellow precipitate soluble with difficulty in \(NH _4 OH\) solution when sodium carbonate extract was first acidified with dil. \(HNO _3\) and then \(AgNO _3\) solution was added. This indicates presence of

[JEE Main 2024, 06 Apr (Shift 2)]

a

\(Br ^{-}\)

b

\(Cl ^{-}\)

c

\(CO _3{ }^{2-}\)

d

\(I ^{-}\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

\(Br ^{-}\)

Explanation

The sodium carbonate extract is first acidified with dilute nitric acid (HNO₃).
This step removes interfering ions like carbonate (CO₃²⁻) and sulfite by converting them into gases (CO₂, SO₂).
Then silver nitrate (AgNO₃) is added to detect halide ions.

If halide ions are present, they form precipitates:

Cl⁻ + Ag⁺ → AgCl (white precipitate)
Br⁻ + Ag⁺ → AgBr (pale yellow precipitate)
I⁻ + Ag⁺ → AgI (deep yellow precipitate)

The question clearly says: Pale yellow precipitate

So: Not Cl⁻ (white), Not I⁻ (deep yellow)

This strongly indicates Br⁻

Confirmation using NH₄OH solubility:

AgCl → dissolves easily
AgBr → dissolves with difficulty (sparingly soluble)
AgI → does not dissolve at all

Given: Pale yellow precipitate, dissolves with difficulty in NH₄OH

Only AgBr satisfies both conditions.

The acidic radical present is: Bromide ion (Br⁻)

Q18

Assertion (A): The gas liberated on warming a salt with dil \(\mathrm{H}_2 \mathrm{SO}_4\), turns a piece of paper dipped in lead acetate into black, it is a confirmatory test for sulphide ion.

Reason (R): In statement-I the colour of paper turns black because of formation of lead sulphite.

[JEE Main 2024, 30 Jan (Shift 1)]

a

Assertion is true but the reason is false

b

Assertion is false but reason is true

c

Both assertion and reason are true and reason is the correct explanation of assertion

d

Both assertion and reason are true but reason is not the correct explanation of assertion

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

Assertion is true but the reason is false

Explanation

Assertion (A):

The gas liberated on warming a salt with dilute H₂SO₄ turns a piece of paper dipped in lead acetate black — confirmatory test for sulphide ion.

True.

When a sulphide salt (like FeS, ZnS, etc.) is treated with dilute H₂SO₄, hydrogen sulphide gas (H₂S) is evolved:

FeS + H2SO4 → FeSO4 + H2S↑

The evolved H₂S gas reacts with lead acetate paper to form black lead sulphide (PbS).

So this is indeed a confirmatory test for sulphide ion (S²⁻).

Reason (R): The colour of paper turns black because of formation of lead sulphite.

False.

The black compound formed is lead sulphide (PbS), not lead sulphite (PbSO₃).

Correct Answer: A

Assertion is true but the reason is false.

Q22

Why NH4Cl is added before NH4OH for the ppt. of Fe3+ ions?

a

To decrease OH-ion concentration

b

To increase Cl-ion concentration

c

To increase NH4+ ion concentration

d

To decrease H+ ion concentration

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

To decrease OH-ion concentration

Explanation

When precipitating Fe³⁺ as Fe(OH)₃, we add NH₄Cl before NH₄OH because:

  • NH₄Cl dissociates to give NH₄⁺ ions.

  • The NH₄⁺ ions suppress the ionization of NH₄OH (common-ion effect).

  • Thus, the concentration of OH⁻ decreases, preventing excess OH⁻ in solution.

This is important because:

  • If OH⁻ concentration becomes too high, other cations (like Zn²⁺, Al³⁺) might also precipitate.

  • Lower OH⁻ ensures selective precipitation of Fe(OH)₃.

Hence, NH₄Cl is added to control (reduce) OH⁻ concentration, giving a clean precipitate.

✔ A is correct.
