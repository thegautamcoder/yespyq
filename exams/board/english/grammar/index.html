Q15 FREE PREVIEW

I have nothing ................... to say.

(Choose the correct option)

a

further

b

neither

c

farther

d

either

✓ Correct answer: a)

further

Explanation

The correct option is A.

Here, 'further' means additional / more. “I have nothing further to say.” means 'I have no more to add'.

Other Options:

  • 'Farther' refers to physical distance (comparative of 'far').

    • Example: We walked farther than we planned.

    • Not correct here because the sentence is about adding information, not distance.

  • 'Neither' and 'Either' are used when making a choice between two things. They do not mean "additional" or "more," so they do not fit the context of this sentence.

Quick Trick to Remember

  • If you can replace the word with 'additional' or 'more', use Further.

  • If you are talking about physical miles/meters, use Farther.

Q16 FREE PREVIEW

He declared his______

(Choose the correct option)

a

innocence

b

innocently

c

innocent

d

by innocent

✓ Correct answer: a)

innocence

Explanation

Here, the verb declared needs a noun object, meaning it should express what he declared.

The word “his” also requires a noun after it. “Innocence” is the noun form of the word and correctly expresses the idea that he claimed he was not guilty.

Other options are incorrect because:

  • “Innocent” is an adjective, so it cannot act as an object.
  • “Innocently” is an adverb that describes a manner, not a thing.
  • “By innocent” is grammatically incorrect and does not make sense in the structure.

Q20 FREE PREVIEW

Q1. How quiet it was!
Instruction: (Turn into assertive)

a

How it was very quiet.

b

It was very quiet.

c

It was really a quiet.

d

It is very quiet.

✓ Correct answer: b)

It was very quiet.

Explanation

(B) It was very quiet. CORRECT. Exclamatory 'How + adj + it was!' → Assertive: remove 'How', use 'It was very + adj.' The intensifier 'very' replaces 'How'.
(A) How it was very quiet. WRONG. This is still an exclamatory structure ('How it was...'). Simply rearranging does not make it assertive. Tense/structure is also unnatural.
(C) It was really a quiet. WRONG. 'Really a quiet' is grammatically incorrect — 'quiet' here is an adjective, not a noun. Cannot say 'a quiet'.
(D) It is very quiet. WRONG. The original sentence is in past tense ('it was'). Option D changes the tense to present ('it is'), which alters the meaning.

Q26 FREE PREVIEW

They went to the beach _________ a picnic.

a

for

b

on

c

at

d

in

✓ Correct answer: a)

for

Explanation

Correct answer : (a) for

The correct sentence is: “They went to the beach for a picnic.” “For a picnic” is used when we mention the purpose of going somewhere. The sentence structure is: go to + place + for + purpose. “On a picnic” is correct only when we say: They went on a picnic. Since the place (the beach) is already mentioned, for is grammatically correct here.
