Arrange the following solutions in order of their increasing boiling points

i10-4M NaClii10-4M Ureaiii10-3M NaCliv10-2M NaCl

a

(ii) < (i) = (iii) < (iv)

(iv) < (iii) < (i) < (ii)

c

(i) < (ii) < (iii) < (iv)

Explanation

∆Tb = iKbm

∆Tb α  i × m

Q2

The density of ' \(x\) ' M solution (' \(x\) ' molar) of NaOH is 1.12 g mL-1, while in molality, the concentration of the solution is 3 m (3 molal). Then \(x\) is
(Given : Molar mass of NaOH is 40 g/mol )

[JEE Main 2024, 6 Apr (Shift 1)]

a

3.8

b

3.5

c

2.8

d

3.0

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

3.0

Explanation

  • Density of solution = 1.12 g/mL

  • Molality (m) = 3 mol/kg

  • Molar mass of NaOH = 40 g/mol

  • Molarity (x) = ?

We are to find the molarity xx (in mol/L) of NaOH.

Molality m=mol of solutemass of solvent in kgm = \frac{\text{mol of solute}}{\text{mass of solvent in kg}}

Given:

Molality=3 mol/kg\text{Molality} = 3 \, \mathrm{mol/kg}

Let’s assume we have 1 kg (1000 g) of water. Then the number of moles of NaOH = 3 mol.

Mass of NaOH =

3 mol×40 g/mol=120 g3 \, \text{mol} \times 40 \, \mathrm{g/mol} = 120 \, \mathrm{g}

So total mass of solution =

1000 g (water)+120 g (NaOH)=1120 g1000 \, \mathrm{g} \text{ (water)} + 120 \, \mathrm{g} \text{ (NaOH)} = 1120 \, \mathrm{g}

Given density = 1.12 g/mL

So for 1120 g of solution:

Volume=1120 g1.12 g/mL=1000 mL=1 L\text{Volume} = \frac{1120 \, \mathrm{g}}{1.12 \, \mathrm{g/mL}} = 1000 \, \mathrm{mL} = 1 \, \mathrm{L}

Molarity x=mol of solutevolume of solution in Lx = \frac{\text{mol of solute}}{\text{volume of solution in L}}

We have 3.0 mol NaOH in 1 L of solution

x = 3.0

Q3

Identify the mixture that shows positive deviations from Raoult's Law

a

CHCl3 + (CH3)2CO

b

(CH3)2CO + C6H5NH2

c

(CH3)2CO + CS2

d

CHCl3 + C6H6

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

(CH3)2CO + CS2

Explanation

Mixing polar acetone with non-polar CS₂ weakens intermolecular attractions (A–B < A–A/B–B) → positive deviation from Raoult’s law.
The other pairs (acetone–chloroform, acetone–aniline, chloroform–benzene) form specific associations/H-bonding → negative deviation.

Q4

1.24 g of AX2 (molar mass 124gmol-1) is dissolved in 1 kg of water to form a solution with boiling point of 100.0156°C, while 25.4 g of AY2 (molar mass 250gmol-1) in 2 kg of water constitutes a solution with a boiling point of 100.0260°C.

KbH2O=0.52Kkgmol-1

Which of the following is correct ?

[JEE Main 2025, 29 Jan (Shift 1)]

a

AX2 and AY2 (both) are completely unionised.

b

AX2 is fully ionised while AY2 is completely unionised.

c

AX2 and AY2 (both) are fully ionised.

d

AX2 is completely unionised while AY2 is fully ionised.

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

AX2 is fully ionised while AY2 is completely unionised.

Explanation

Calculate Van't Hoff Factor (i) for AX2

ΔTb=i×Kb×m 0.0156=i×0.52×0.01 i=0.01560.0052=3

Since i=3,AX2 fully ionises
Calculate Van't Hoff Factor (i) for AY2

ΔTb=i×Kb×m 0.0260=i×0.52×0.0508 i=0.02600.0264=≈1

Since i≈1,AY2 does not ionise (remains unionised).

Q5

The Henry's law constant KH values of three gases (A, B, C) in water are 145, 2×10-5 and 35 kbar, respectively. The solubility of these gases in water follow the order

a

A>B>C

b

B>A>C

c

B>C>A

d

A>C>B

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

B>C>A

Explanation

According to Henry’s law,
Lower KH means higher solubility.
Given:

  • Kₕ(B) = 2 × 10⁻⁵ (lowest) → highest solubility

  • Kₕ(C) = 35

  • Kₕ(A) = 145 (highest) → lowest solubility
    So, B > C > A.

Q7

The Molarity (M) of an aqueous solution containing 5.85 g of NaCl in 500 mL water is (Given : Molar Mass Na: 23 and Cl: 35.5gmol-1)

[JEE Main 2024, 4 Apr (Shift 1)]

a

0.2

b

4

c

20

d

2

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

0.2

Explanation

- Mass of NaCl=5.85 g
- Volume of water =500 mL=0.5 L
- Molar Mass of Na(Na)=23 g/mol
- Molar Mass of Cl(Cl)=35.5 g/mol
1. Calculate the molar mass of NaCl :
- Molar mass of NaCl=23+35.5=58.5 g/mol
2. Calculate the number of moles of NaCl :
- Number of moles = mass ofNaCl/ molar mass of NaCl \(=5.85 \mathrm{~g} / 58.5 \mathrm{~g} / \mathrm{mol}\) \(=0.1\) moles
3. Convert the volume of the solution from mL to L :
- Volume \(=\mathbf{5 0 0 ~ m L}=\mathbf{0 . 5 ~ L}\)
4. Calculate the molarity (M):
- Molarity (M)= number of moles of solute \(/\) volume of solution in liters \(=0.1\) =0.2M
