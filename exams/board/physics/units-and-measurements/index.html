Statement II: The vernier constant is given by one main scale division multiplied by the number of vernier scale divisions.
In the light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below.

a

Statement I is false but Statement II is true

b

Statement I is true but Statement II is false

d

Statement I is true but Statement II is false

Explanation

A vernier scale is designed such that its divisions are slightly smaller than the main scale divisions. This difference allows precise measurements by aligning a particular vernier division with the main scale.

Explanation

Magnetic field B :
The dimensional formula for B is derived from the Lorentz force law:

\(
F=q v B \quad \Rightarrow \quad B=\frac{F}{q v}
\)

1. Force (F) has the dimensional formula:

\(
[F]=\left[\mathrm{MLT}^{-2}\right]
\)

2. Charge (q) has the dimensional formula:

\(
[q]=[\mathrm{AT}]
\)

3. Velocity (v) has the dimensional formula:

\(
[v]=\left[\mathrm{LT}^{-1}\right]
\)


Thus, the dimensional formula for B is:

\(
[B]=\frac{\left[\mathrm{MLT}^{-2}\right]}{[\mathrm{AT}]\left[\mathrm{LT}^{-1}\right]}=\frac{[\mathrm{M}]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]}{[\mathrm{A}]}=[\mathrm{M}]\left[\mathrm{A}^{-1}\right]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]
\)


Magnetic permeability ( \(\mu_0\) ):
The dimensional formula of \(\mu_0\) is:

\(
\left[\mu_0\right]=[\mathrm{M}][\mathrm{L}]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]\left[\mathrm{A}^{-2}\right]
\)


Dimensions of \(\frac{B}{\mu_0}\) :
Using the formula:

\(
\frac{B}{\mu_0}=\frac{[\mathrm{M}]\left[\mathrm{A}^{-1}\right]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]}{[\mathrm{M}][\mathrm{L}]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]\left[\mathrm{A}^{-2}\right]}
\)


Cancel the terms:

\(
\frac{B}{\mu_0}=[\mathrm{A}]\left[\mathrm{L}^{-1}\right]
\)

✓ Correct answer: a)

Angular momentum and Planck's constant

Explanation

We analyze the dimensions of each pair of physical quantities to determine which have the same dimensions.

Option A: Angular Momentum and Planck's Constant

  • Angular momentum (LL):

    L=mvr=mass×velocity×distanceL = mvr = \text{mass} \times \text{velocity} \times \text{distance} =[M][LT−1][L]=[ML2T−1]= [M] [LT^{-1}] [L] = [M L^2 T^{-1}]

  • Planck's constant (hh):
    Planck’s constant is defined in the equation:

    E=hfE = h f

    where EE is energy and ff is frequency.
    Since energy has dimension [ML2T−2][M L^2 T^{-2}] and frequency [T−1][T^{-1}], we get:

    [h]=[E][f]=[ML2T−2][T−1]=[ML2T−1][h] = \frac{[E]}{[f]} = \frac{[M L^2 T^{-2}]}{[T^{-1}]} = [M L^2 T^{-1}]

Both have the same dimensions: [ML2T−1][M L^2 T^{-1}].

Option B: Torque and Moment of Inertia

  • Torque (τ\tau):

    τ=r×F=[L]×[MLT−2]=[ML2T−2]\tau = r \times F = [L] \times [M L T^{-2}] = [M L^2 T^{-2}]

  • Moment of inertia (II):

    I=mr2=[M][L2]=[ML2]I = m r^2 = [M] [L^2] = [M L^2]

Different dimensions.

Option C: Impulse and Surface Tension

  • Impulse (JJ):

    J=Force×time=[MLT−2][T]=[MLT−1]J = \text{Force} \times \text{time} = [M L T^{-2}] [T] = [M L T^{-1}]

  • Surface tension (TT):

    T=ForceLength=[MLT−2][L]=[MT−2]T = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Length}} = \frac{[M L T^{-2}]}{[L]} = [M T^{-2}]

Different dimensions.

Option D: Momentum and Work Done

  • Momentum (pp):

    p=mv=[M][LT−1]=[MLT−1]p = m v = [M] [L T^{-1}] = [M L T^{-1}]

  • Work Done (WW):

    W=Force×Distance=[MLT−2][L]=[ML2T−2]W = \text{Force} \times \text{Distance} = [M L T^{-2}] [L] = [M L^2 T^{-2}]

Different dimensions.

Final Answer:

AAngular momentum and Planck’s constant\boxed{A} \quad \text{Angular momentum and Planck's constant}

Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Explanation

The formula for percentage error propagation in a function is:

ΔE%=∣∂E∂x∣Δx%+∣∂E∂t∣Δt%\Delta E_{\%} = \left| \frac{\partial E}{\partial x} \right| \Delta x_{\%} + \left| \frac{\partial E}{\partial t} \right| \Delta t_{\%}​

Given:

E(x,t)=x3e−βtE(x, t) = x^3 e^{-\beta t} Step 1: Find percentage errors due to xx and tt

  1. For x3x^3: The percentage error for x3x^3 is:

    ΔE% x=3⋅Δx%\Delta E_{\% \, x} = 3 \cdot \Delta x_{\%}​

    Substituting Δx%=1.2%\Delta x_{\%} = 1.2\%

    ΔE% x=3⋅1.2=3.6%\Delta E_{\% \, x} = 3 \cdot 1.2 = 3.6\%

  2. For e−βte^{-\beta t}: The percentage error due to ttt comes from the exponential term. Using:

    ΔE% t=β⋅t⋅Δt%\Delta E_{\% \, t} = \beta \cdot t \cdot \Delta t_{\%}​

    Substituting β=0.3 s−1,t=5 s,Δt%=1.6%\beta = 0.3 \, \text{s}^{-1}, t = 5 \, \text{s}, \Delta t_{\%} = 1.6\%

    ΔE% t=0.3⋅5⋅1.6=2.4%\Delta E_{\% \, t} = 0.3 \cdot 5 \cdot 1.6 = 2.4\%

The total percentage error in EEE is:

ΔE%=ΔE% x+ΔE% t\Delta E_{\%} = \Delta E_{\% \, x} + \Delta E_{\% \, t}​

Substituting the values:

ΔE%=3.6+2.4=6.0%\Delta E_{\%} = 3.6 + 2.4 = 6.0\%

a

[AL]

b

AL-1

c

[MAL]

MALT-1

Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

AL-1

Explanation

Magnetic field B :
The dimensional formula for B is derived from the Lorentz force law:

\(
F=q v B \quad \Rightarrow \quad B=\frac{F}{q v}
\)

1. Force (F) has the dimensional formula:

\(
[F]=\left[\mathrm{MLT}^{-2}\right]
\)

2. Charge (q) has the dimensional formula:

\(
[q]=[\mathrm{AT}]
\)

3. Velocity (v) has the dimensional formula:

\(
[v]=\left[\mathrm{LT}^{-1}\right]
\)


Thus, the dimensional formula for B is:

\(
[B]=\frac{\left[\mathrm{MLT}^{-2}\right]}{[\mathrm{AT}]\left[\mathrm{LT}^{-1}\right]}=\frac{[\mathrm{M}]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]}{[\mathrm{A}]}=[\mathrm{M}]\left[\mathrm{A}^{-1}\right]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]
\)


Magnetic permeability ( \(\mu_0\) ):
The dimensional formula of \(\mu_0\) is:

\(
\left[\mu_0\right]=[\mathrm{M}][\mathrm{L}]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]\left[\mathrm{A}^{-2}\right]
\)


Dimensions of \(\frac{B}{\mu_0}\) :
Using the formula:

\(
\frac{B}{\mu_0}=\frac{[\mathrm{M}]\left[\mathrm{A}^{-1}\right]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]}{[\mathrm{M}][\mathrm{L}]\left[\mathrm{T}^{-2}\right]\left[\mathrm{A}^{-2}\right]}
\)


Cancel the terms:

\(
\frac{B}{\mu_0}=[\mathrm{A}]\left[\mathrm{L}^{-1}\right]
\)
