Q6

A disc of mass MM and radius RR is rotating about its axis. If the angle rotated about it as a function of time ttt is θ=at2+bt+c\theta = at^2 + bt + c, where a,b,a, b, and cc are constants, find the power derived to the disc as a function of time.

a

aMR2(2at+b)aMR^2 (2at + b)

b

aMR2aMR^2

c

2a2MR2t2a^2MR^2t

d

aMR2baMR^2 b

🔒
Answer & explanation — Premium

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

aMR2(2at+b)aMR^2 (2at + b)

Explanation

  1. Expression for Torque and Power: The power derived to the disc is given by:

    P=τ⋅ωP = \tau \cdot \omega

    where τ\tau is the torque, and ω\omega is the angular velocity.

  2. Moment of Inertia: For a disc rotating about its axis:

    I=12MR2I = \frac{1}{2} M R^2

  3. Angular Velocity (ω\omega): Angular velocity is the derivative of angle θ\theta with respect to time:

    ω=dθdt=ddt(at2+bt+c)\omega = \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt} \left( at^2 + bt + c \right), ω=2at+b\omega = 2at + b

  4. Angular Acceleration (α\alpha): Angular acceleration is the derivative of angular velocity:

    α=dωdt=ddt(2at+b)\alpha = \frac{d\omega}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt} \left( 2at + b \right), α=2a\alpha = 2a

  5. Torque (τ\tauτ): Torque is related to angular acceleration:

    τ=I⋅α\tau = I \cdot \alpha

    Substitute I=12MR2I = \frac{1}{2} M R^2 and α=2a\alpha = 2a

    τ=12MR2⋅2a=aMR2\tau = \frac{1}{2} M R^2 \cdot 2a = a M R^2

  6. Power (PP): Substitute τ=aMR2\tau = a M R^2 and ω=2at+b\omega = 2at + b into the power formula:

    P=τ⋅ω=(aMR2)⋅(2at+b)P = \tau \cdot \omega = (a M R^2) \cdot (2at + b), P=aMR2(2at+b)P = a M R^2 (2at + b)
Final Answer:

The power derived to the disc as a function of time is:

aMR2(2at+b)\boxed{a M R^2 (2at + b)}​
