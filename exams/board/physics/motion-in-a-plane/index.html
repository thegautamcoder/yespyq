Q1

A particle is projected with a velocity 60 m/s60 \, \text{m/s} at an angle 30∘30^\circ with respect to the horizontal. It reaches a height h1h_1​ in the first second and height h2h_2​ in the last second during its upward motion. Find the ratio of h1h2\frac{h_1}{h_2}. (JEE Mains - 21 Jan 2025 - Shift I Memory Based)

a

4:1

b

2:1

c

5:1

d

3:1

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

5:1

Explanation

  1. Initial Vertical Velocity (uyu_y):

    uy=usin⁡θ=60sin⁡30∘=30 m/s.u_y = u \sin \theta = 60 \sin 30^\circ = 30 \, \text{m/s}.

  2. Time of Flight (Total time of upward motion):
    Using the formula for the total time of motion upward:

    Tup=uyg=3010=3 seconds.T_{\text{up}} = \frac{u_y}{g} = \frac{30}{10} = 3 \, \text{seconds}.

    The last second in the upward motion corresponds to the time t=2 seconds→3 secondst = 2 \, \text{seconds} \to 3 \, \text{seconds}

Height in the First Second (h1h_1​):

Using the equation of motion:

h=uyt−12gt2,h = u_y t - \frac{1}{2} g t^2,

for t=1t = 1:

h1=(30)(1)−12(10)(1)2=30−5=25 m.h_1 = (30)(1) - \frac{1}{2}(10)(1)^2 = 30 - 5 = 25 \, \text{m}.

Height in the Last Second Upward (h2h_2):

For the last second in the upward motion, the time interval is t=2→3 secondst = 2 \to 3 \, \text{seconds}. To find h2h_2, calculate the difference in height between these two time intervals:

h2=Height at t=3 seconds−Height at t=2 seconds.h_2 = \text{Height at } t = 3 \, \text{seconds} - \text{Height at } t = 2 \, \text{seconds}.

Height at t=2t = 2:

h(t=2)=(30)(2)−12(10)(2)2=60−20=40 m.h(t=2) = (30)(2) - \frac{1}{2}(10)(2)^2 = 60 - 20 = 40 \, \text{m}.

Height at t=3t = 3:

h(t=3)=(30)(3)−12(10)(3)2=90−45=45 m.h(t=3) = (30)(3) - \frac{1}{2}(10)(3)^2 = 90 - 45 = 45 \, \text{m}.

Height in the last second:

h2=h(t=3)−h(t=2)=45−40=5 m.h_2 = h(t=3) - h(t=2) = 45 - 40 = 5 \, \text{m}.

Ratio h1h2\frac{h_1}{h_2}​​:

h1h2=255=5:1.\frac{h_1}{h_2} = \frac{25}{5} = 5:1.

Final Answer:

5:1\boxed{5:1}​

Q3

If the radius of curvature of the path of two particles of same mass are in the ratio 3:4, then in order to have constant centripetal force, their velocities will be in the ratio of:

a

3 : 2

b

1 : 3

c

3: 1

d

2 : 3

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

3 : 2

Explanation

r1r2 =34

As centripetal force F =mv2r
In order to have constant (same in this question) centripetal force

\begin{aligned}
& F_1=F_2 \\
& \frac{m_1 v_1^2}{r_1}=\frac{m_2 v_2^2}{r_2} \\
& \Rightarrow \frac{v_1}{v_2}=\sqrt{\frac{r_1}{r_2}}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\end{aligned}

Q6

Two particles are projected with the same velocity but at different projection angles: π4+α​ and π4-α. Determine the ratio of their maximum heights.​

a

1+sin⁡2α1−sin⁡2α\frac{1 + \sin 2\alpha}{1 - \sin 2\alpha}​

b

1−sin⁡2α1+sin⁡2α\frac{1 - \sin 2\alpha}{1 + \sin 2\alpha}​

c

1+tan⁡2α1−tan⁡2α\frac{1 + \tan 2\alpha}{1 - \tan 2\alpha}​

d

1−cos⁡2α1+cos⁡2α\frac{1 - \cos 2\alpha}{1 + \cos 2\alpha}​

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

1+sin⁡2α1−sin⁡2α\frac{1 + \sin 2\alpha}{1 - \sin 2\alpha}​

ExplanationStep 1: Formula for Maximum Height

The maximum height of a projectile is given by:

H=u2sin⁡2θ2gH = \frac{u^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g}​

where:

  • uu is the initial velocity,
  • θ\theta is the angle of projection,
  • gg is the acceleration due to gravity.
Step 2: Maximum Heights of Given Angles

The two angles of projection given are:

  1. θ1=α+π4\theta_1 = \alpha + \frac{\pi}{4}​
  2. θ2=α−π4\theta_2 = \alpha - \frac{\pi}{4}​

Using the formula for maximum height:

H1=u2sin⁡2(α+π/4)2gH_1 = \frac{u^2 \sin^2(\alpha + \pi/4)}{2g}

H2=u2sin⁡2(α−π/4)2gH_2 = \frac{u^2 \sin^2(\alpha - \pi/4)}{2g}​

Step 3: Taking the Ratio

The required ratio is:

H1H2=sin⁡2(α+π/4)sin⁡2(α−π/4)\frac{H_1}{H_2} = \frac{\sin^2(\alpha + \pi/4)}{\sin^2(\alpha - \pi/4)}​

Using the sine angle addition and subtraction formulas:

sin⁡(α+π/4)=sin⁡α+cos⁡α2\sin(\alpha + \pi/4) = \frac{\sin \alpha + \cos \alpha}{\sqrt{2}}

​​ sin⁡(α−π/4)=sin⁡α−cos⁡α2\sin(\alpha - \pi/4) = \frac{\sin \alpha - \cos \alpha}{\sqrt{2}}​

Squaring both:

sin⁡2(α+π/4)=(sin⁡α+cos⁡α)22\sin^2(\alpha + \pi/4) = \frac{(\sin \alpha + \cos \alpha)^2}{2}

sin⁡2(α−π/4)=(sin⁡α−cos⁡α)22\sin^2(\alpha - \pi/4) = \frac{(\sin \alpha - \cos \alpha)^2}{2}​

Thus, the ratio simplifies to:

H1H2=(sin⁡α+cos⁡α)2(sin⁡α−cos⁡α)2\frac{H_1}{H_2} = \frac{(\sin \alpha + \cos \alpha)^2}{(\sin \alpha - \cos \alpha)^2}​

Using the identity:

(sin⁡α+cos⁡α)2=1+sin⁡2α(\sin \alpha + \cos \alpha)^2 = 1 + \sin 2\alpha

(sin⁡α−cos⁡α)2=1−sin⁡2α(\sin \alpha - \cos \alpha)^2 = 1 - \sin 2\alpha

H1H2=1+sin⁡2α1−sin⁡2α\frac{H_1}{H_2} = \frac{1 + \sin 2\alpha}{1 - \sin 2\alpha}​

Step 4: Selecting the Correct Option

Comparing with the given options, the correct answer is:

A1+sin⁡2α1−sin⁡2α\boxed{A} \quad \frac{1 + \sin 2\alpha}{1 - \sin 2\alpha}​

a

sin−12gT2π2R12

b

sin−1π2r2gT212

c

cos−12gT2π2R12

d

cos−1πR2gT212

✓ Correct answer: a)

sin−12gT2π2R12

Explanation

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
& \text { Maximum height } H=\frac{v^2 \sin ^2 \theta}{2 g} \\
& 4 R=\frac{4 \pi^2 R^2}{T^2 2 g} \sin ^2 \theta \quad\left[\therefore \frac{2 \pi R}{T}=V\right] \\
& \Rightarrow \sin \theta=\sqrt{\frac{2 g T^2}{\pi^2 R}} \\
& \Rightarrow \theta=\sin ^{-1}\left(\frac{2 g T^2}{\pi^2 R}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Q9

If the angles of projection for two projectiles are 30∘30^\circ and 60∘60^\circ, what is the ratio of their velocities at maximum height?

a

1:21:2

b

2:12:1

c

3:1\sqrt{3}:1

d

1:31:\sqrt{3}

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

3:1\sqrt{3}:1

Explanation

. The velocity at maximum height for a projectile is equal to its horizontal velocity, which remains constant throughout the motion. Horizontal velocity is given by vx=ucos⁡θv_x = u \cos\theta

For θ=30∘\theta = 30^\circ: vx1=ucos⁡30∘=u32v_{x1} = u \cos 30^\circ = u \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}​​.
For θ=60∘\theta = 60^\circ:vx2=ucos⁡60∘=u12v_{x2} = u \cos 60^\circ = u \frac{1}{2}.

The ratio of velocities at maximum height is:

vx1vx2=u⋅32u⋅12=3:1.\frac{v_{x1}}{v_{x2}} = \frac{u \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{u \cdot \frac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{3}:1.

Thus, the correct answer is (c) 3:1\sqrt{3}:1
