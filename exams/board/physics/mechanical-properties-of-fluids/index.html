Q1

Find the pressure difference of an air bubble of radius 2 cm formed 20 cm below an open water surface and atmospheric pressure.
(Given surface tension of water = 70×10−3 N/m70 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{N/m})(Shift - II Memory Based)

a

0.3×105 N/m20.3 \times 10^5 \, \text{N/m}^2

b

0.02×105 N/m2

c

2.03×105 N/m22.03 \times 10^5 \, \text{N/m}^2

d

1.03×105 N/m21.03 \times 10^5 \, \text{N/m}^2

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

0.02×105 N/m2

Explanation

The pressure difference inside an air bubble submerged in water is due to two contributions:

  1. Due to surface tension: ΔPsurface tension=2Tr\Delta P_{\text{surface tension}} = \frac{2T}{r}​, where TT is the surface tension and rr is the radius of the bubble.
  2. Due to water column: ΔPwater=ρgh\Delta P_{\text{water}} = \rho g h, where ρ\rho is the density of water, gg is gravitational acceleration, and hh is the depth of the bubble below the surface.
Step 1: Calculate Pressure Difference Due to Surface Tension

ΔPsurface tension=2Tr\Delta P_{\text{surface tension}} = \frac{2T}{r}​

Given:

T=70×10−3 N/m,r=2 cm=0.02 mT = 70 \times 10^{-3} \, \text{N/m}, \quad r = 2 \, \text{cm} = 0.02 \, \text{m},

ΔPsurface tension=2×70×10−30.02\Delta P_{\text{surface tension}} = \frac{2 \times 70 \times 10^{-3}}{0.02},

​ ΔPsurface tension=7 N/m2\Delta P_{\text{surface tension}} = 7 \, \text{N/m}^2

Step 2: Calculate Pressure Difference Due to Water Column

ΔPwater=ρgh\Delta P_{\text{water}} = \rho g h

Given:

ρ=1000 kg/m3,g=9.8 m/s2,h=20 cm=0.2 m\rho = 1000 \, \text{kg/m}^3, \quad g = 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2, \quad h = 20 \, \text{cm} = 0.2 \, \text{m}

ΔPwater=1000×9.8×0.2\Delta P_{\text{water}} = 1000 \times 9.8 \times 0.2

ΔPwater=1960 N/m2

Step 3: Total Pressure Difference

The total pressure difference is the sum of the two contributions:

ΔP=ΔPsurface tension+ΔPwater\Delta P = \Delta P_{\text{surface tension}} + \Delta P_{\text{water}}

ΔP=7+1960=1967 N/m2

Convert to standard form:

ΔP=0.01967×105 N/m2≈0.02×105 N/m2

Final Answer:

(b) 0.02×105 N/m2​

Q5

A sphere of mass MM is dropped into glycerin. The density of glycerin is half the density of the sphere. After some time, the sphere moves down with constant velocity. What is the viscous force acting on the sphere?

(Shift II Memory Based)​

a

Mg2\frac{Mg}{2}​

b

Mg3\frac{Mg}{3}​

c

Mg4\frac{Mg}{4}​

d

​Mg6\frac{Mg}{6}​

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: a)

Mg2\frac{Mg}{2}​

ExplanationKey Concept:

At terminal velocity (constant velocity), the net force acting on the sphere is zero. The forces acting on the sphere are:

  1. Gravitational Force (FgF_g): Downward force due to the weight of the sphere. Fg=MgF_g = Mg
  2. Buoyant Force (FbF_b​): Upward force due to the displaced glycerin. Fb=ρglycerinVgF_b = \rho_{\text{glycerin}} V g where ρglycerin\rho_{\text{glycerin}}is the density of glycerin, VV is the volume of the sphere, and gg is the acceleration due to gravity.
  3. Viscous Force (FvF_v​): Upward force due to the viscosity of glycerin. This balances the remaining downward force at terminal velocity.

From equilibrium at terminal velocity:

Fv=Fg−FbF_v = F_g - F_b​

Step 1: Relationship Between Densities

The density of glycerin (ρglycerin\rho_{\text{glycerin}}) is half the density of the sphere (ρsphere\rho_{\text{sphere}}):

ρglycerin=ρsphere2\rho_{\text{glycerin}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{sphere}}}{2}​​

The mass MM of the sphere is related to its density and volume:

M=ρsphereVM = \rho_{\text{sphere}} V

Substitute ρglycerin=ρsphere2\rho_{\text{glycerin}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{sphere}}}{2}​​ into FbF_b:

Fb=ρsphere2Vg=M2gF_b = \frac{\rho_{\text{sphere}}}{2} V g = \frac{M}{2} g

Step 2: Calculate the Viscous Force

Using Fv=Fg−FbF_v = F_g - F_b:

Fv=Mg−M2gF_v = Mg - \frac{M}{2}g

Simplify:

Fv=M2gF_v = \frac{M}{2} g

Final Answer:

The viscous force acting on the sphere is:

Mg2\boxed{\frac{Mg}{2}}​​

Q11

A bubble of radius R is split into 27 drops of equal radius, and the work done is 10J. If the same bubble is split into 64 equal drops, the work done is:

(Shift 1 Memory Based)

a

10 J

b

15 J

c

20 J

d

25 J

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

15 J

Explanation

The work done is proportional to the change in surface area. The surface area of a sphere is 4πr24 \pi r^2.

  1. Initial bubble area:
    The radius of the bubble is RR. Its surface area is:

    Ainitial=4πR2A_{\text{initial}} = 4 \pi R^2

  2. For 27 drops:

    • Each drop has radius r1r_1 such that the volume of the bubble equals the total volume of the drops: 43πR3=27⋅43πr13  ⟹  r1=R3\frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 = 27 \cdot \frac{4}{3} \pi r_1^3 \implies r_1 = \frac{R}{3}​
    • Total surface area of 27 drops: A27 drops=27⋅4πr12=27⋅4π(R3)2=12πR2A_{\text{27 drops}} = 27 \cdot 4 \pi r_1^2 = 27 \cdot 4 \pi \left( \frac{R}{3} \right)^2 = 12 \pi R^2
    • Change in surface area: ΔA=A27 drops−Ainitial=12πR2−4πR2=8πR2\Delta A = A_{\text{27 drops}} - A_{\text{initial}} = 12 \pi R^2 - 4 \pi R^2 = 8 \pi R^2
    • Work done is proportional to ΔA\Delta A: W1=kΔA  ⟹  k=108πR2W_1 = k \Delta A \implies k = \frac{10}{8 \pi R^2}​

  3. For 64 drops:

    • Each drop has radius r2r_2​ such that: 43πR3=64⋅43πr23  ⟹  r2=R4\frac{4}{3} \pi R^3 = 64 \cdot \frac{4}{3} \pi r_2^3 \implies r_2 = \frac{R}{4}​
    • Total surface area of 64 drops: A64 drops=64⋅4πr22=64⋅4π(R4)2=16πR2A_{\text{64 drops}} = 64 \cdot 4 \pi r_2^2 = 64 \cdot 4 \pi \left( \frac{R}{4} \right)^2 = 16 \pi R^2
    • Change in surface area: ΔA=A64 drops−Ainitial=16πR2−4πR2=12πR2\Delta A = A_{\text{64 drops}} - A_{\text{initial}} = 16 \pi R^2 - 4 \pi R^2 = 12 \pi R^2
    • Work done: W2=kΔA=108πR2⋅12πR2=15 JW_2 = k \Delta A = \frac{10}{8 \pi R^2} \cdot 12 \pi R^2 = 15 \, \text{J}

Thus, the work done when splitting into 64 drops is 15 J.

(Shift - II Memory Based)​

a

VA−VB=ρ2(PB2−PA2)V_A - V_B = \frac{\rho}{2(P_B^2 - P_A^2)}​

b

VA−VB=2(PA−PB)ρV_A - V_B = \frac{2(P_A - P_B)}{\rho}​

c

​VA2−VB2=2(PB−PA)ρV_A^2 - V_B^2 = \frac{2(P_B - P_A)}{\rho}​

VA2−VB2=2(PA−PB)ρV_A^2 - V_B^2 = \frac{2(P_A - P_B)}{\rho}​

✓ Correct answer: c)

​VA2−VB2=2(PB−PA)ρV_A^2 - V_B^2 = \frac{2(P_B - P_A)}{\rho}​

Explanation

This question is based on Bernoulli's equation, which states:

P+12ρV2+ρgh=constantP + \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 + \rho g h = \text{constant}

Since the pipe is horizontal (hhh is constant), the equation simplifies to:

P+12ρV2=constantP + \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 = \text{constant}

For points A and B:

PA+12ρVA2=PB+12ρVB2P_A + \frac{1}{2} \rho V_A^2 = P_B + \frac{1}{2} \rho V_B^2​

Rearranging:

12ρVA2−12ρVB2=PB−PA, ​ ρ2(VA2−VB2)=PB−PA​

Divide through by ρ/2\rho/2:

VA2−VB2=2(PB−PA)ρ​ Final Relation:

The correct option is:

VA2−VB2=2(PB−PA)ρ
