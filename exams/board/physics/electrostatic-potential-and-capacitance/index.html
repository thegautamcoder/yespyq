Q1 FREE PREVIEW

A capacitor with capacitance \(1 \mu \mathrm{~F}\) is connected to a 20 V supply. The distance between the plates is 1 \(\mu \mathrm{m}\). Find the energy density between the plates.

(Shift - II Memory Based)

a

\(1770 \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^3\)

b

\(1800 \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^3\)

c

\(1600 \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^3\)

d

\(2000 \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^3\)

✓ Correct answer: a)

\(1770 \mathrm{~J} / \mathrm{m}^3\)

Explanation

To find the energy density (energy per unit volume) between the plates of the capacitor, we use the formula:

Energy Density(u)=12ϵ0E2\text{Energy Density} (u) = \frac{1}{2} \epsilon_0 E^2

where:

  • ϵ0\epsilon_0​ = Permittivity of free space =8.85×10−12 F/m= 8.85 \times 10^{-12} \, F/m
  • EE = Electric field inside the capacitor, given by:

E=VdE = \frac{V}{d}​

Step 1: Calculate the Electric Field EE

E=Vd=20V1×10−6mE = \frac{V}{d} = \frac{20V}{1 \times 10^{-6} m}

E=2×107 V/mE = 2 \times 10^7 \, V/m

Step 2: Calculate the Energy Density uu

u=12×(8.85×10−12)×(2×107)2u = \frac{1}{2} \times (8.85 \times 10^{-12}) \times (2 \times 10^7)^2

u=12×(8.85×10−12)×(4×1014)u = \frac{1}{2} \times (8.85 \times 10^{-12}) \times (4 \times 10^{14})

u=12×3.54×103u = \frac{1}{2} \times 3.54 \times 10^3

u=1.77×103 J/m3u = 1.77 \times 10^3 \, J/m^3

Final Answer:

1770J/m3

So, the correct option is (A) 1770 J/m³.

Q4 FREE PREVIEW

A capacitor \(C_1=6 \mu \mathrm{~F}\), initially charged with a cell of emf 5 V is disconnected and connected to another capacitor \(C_2=12 \mu \mathrm{~F}\) which is initially neutral. The charges on \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) after connection are

(Shift - II Memory Based)

a

\(0 \mu \mathrm{C}, 30 \mu \mathrm{C}\)

b

\(10 \mu \mathrm{C}, 20 \mu \mathrm{C}\)

c

\(20 \mu \mathrm{C}, 10 \mu \mathrm{C}\)

d

\(30 \mu \mathrm{C}, 0 \mu \mathrm{C}\)

✓ Correct answer: b)

\(10 \mu \mathrm{C}, 20 \mu \mathrm{C}\)

ExplanationStep 1: Initial Charge on C1C_1​

The charge on a capacitor is given by:

Q=CVQ = C V

Given:

  • C1=6μFC_1 = 6 \mu F
  • V=5VV = 5V

Q1=(6×10−6F)×(5V)=30μCQ_1 = (6 \times 10^{-6} F) \times (5V) = 30 \mu C

Since C2C_2​ is initially uncharged, its initial charge is:

Q2=0μCQ_2 = 0 \mu C

Step 2: Charge Conservation

When the two capacitors are connected, charge redistributes while maintaining charge conservation:

Qtotal=Q1+Q2=30μC+0=30μCQ_{\text{total}} = Q_1 + Q_2 = 30 \mu C + 0 = 30 \mu C

Step 3: Common Voltage after Connection

Since the capacitors are connected in parallel, they share a common voltage VfV_f:

Vf=QtotalC1+C2V_f = \frac{Q_{\text{total}}}{C_1 + C_2}

Vf=30μC(6+12)μFV_f = \frac{30 \mu C}{(6 + 12) \mu F}V

​ Vf=3018V=53VV_f = \frac{30}{18} V = \frac{5}{3} V

Step 4: Finding Final Charges

Using Q=CVQ = CV

  1. Final charge on C1C_1:

Q1′=C1Vf=(6μF)×53VQ_1' = C_1 V_f = (6 \mu F) \times \frac{5}{3} V

Q1′=10μCQ_1' = 10 \mu C

  1. Final charge on C2C_2:

Q2′=C2Vf=(12μF)×53VQ_2' = C_2 V_f = (12 \mu F) \times \frac{5}{3} V

Q2′=20μCQ_2' = 20 \mu C

Step 5: Selecting the Correct Option

The charges on C1C_1​ and C2C_2​ after connection are:

B10μC,20μC\boxed{B} \quad 10 \mu C, 20 \mu C
