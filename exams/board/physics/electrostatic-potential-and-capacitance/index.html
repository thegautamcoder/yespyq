Explanation

To find the energy density (energy per unit volume) between the plates of the capacitor, we use the formula:

where:

  • EE = Electric field inside the capacitor, given by:

E=VdE = \frac{V}{d}​

Step 1: Calculate the Electric Field EE

E=Vd=20V1×10−6mE = \frac{V}{d} = \frac{20V}{1 \times 10^{-6} m}

E=2×107 V/mE = 2 \times 10^7 \, V/m

u=12×(8.85×10−12)×(2×107)2u = \frac{1}{2} \times (8.85 \times 10^{-12}) \times (2 \times 10^7)^2

u=12×(8.85×10−12)×(4×1014)u = \frac{1}{2} \times (8.85 \times 10^{-12}) \times (4 \times 10^{14})

u=12×3.54×103u = \frac{1}{2} \times 3.54 \times 10^3

u=1.77×103 J/m3u = 1.77 \times 10^3 \, J/m^3

Final Answer:

1770J/m3

✓ Correct answer: c)

4 mC and 0.2 J

Explanation

  1. Initial Charge Stored:
    The charge stored in a capacitor is given by:

    Q=CVQ = CV

    Substituting C=40 μFC = 40 \, \mu \text{F} and V=100 VV = 100 \, \text{V}:

    Q=40×10−6×100=4 mC.Q = 40 \times 10^{-6} \times 100 = 4 \, \text{mC}.

  2. Capacitance with Dielectric:
    When the dielectric is introduced, the capacitance becomes:

    C′=K⋅C=2⋅40=80 μF.C' = K \cdot C = 2 \cdot 40 = 80 \, \mu \text{F}.

  3. Charge with Dielectric:

    Q′=C′⋅V=80×10−6⋅100=8 mC.Q' = C' \cdot V = 80 \times 10^{-6} \cdot 100 = 8 \, \text{mC}.

    The extra charge stored is:

    ΔQ=Q′−Q=8−4=4 mC.\Delta Q = Q' - Q = 8 - 4 = 4 \, \text{mC}.

  4. Change in Electrostatic Energy:
    The energy stored in a capacitor is given by:

    U=12CV2.U = \frac{1}{2} CV^2.
    • Initial energy: U=12⋅40×10−6⋅(100)2=0.2 J.U = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 40 \times 10^{-6} \cdot (100)^2 = 0.2 \, \text{J}.
    • Final energy with dielectric: U′=12⋅80×10−6⋅(100)2=0.4 J.U' = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 80 \times 10^{-6} \cdot (100)^2 = 0.4 \, \text{J}.

    The change in energy is:

    ΔU=U′−U=0.4−0.2=0.2 J.\Delta U = U' - U = 0.4 - 0.2 = 0.2 \, \text{J}.

Thus, the extra charge stored is 4 mC4 \, \text{mC} and the change in energy is 0.2 J.

Explanation

Analyzing the influence of multiple point charges at a distance. At large distances, the individual effects of point charges q1​,q2​,q3​ on the potential can be considered to merge into a single effect similar to that of a single point charge with their cumulative charge. Q13

A parallel plate capacitor is charged by a battery. The battery is then disconnected and the plates of the charged capacitor are then moved farther apart. In the process :

a

the charge on the capacitor increases.

b

the potential difference across the plates decreases.

c

the capacitance of the capacitor increases.

d

the electrostatic energy stored in the capacitor increases.

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

the electrostatic energy stored in the capacitor increases.

Explanation

Battery is disconnected, so \(\mathbf{Q = \text{constant}}\).

Capacitance (C): \(C = \frac{\epsilon_0 A}{d}\)
As \(d\) (separation) increases, \(C\) decreases.
Potential Difference (V): \(V = \frac{Q}{C}\)
As \(Q\) is constant and \(C\) decreases, \(V\) increases.
Electrostatic Energy (U): \(U = \frac{Q^2}{2C}\)
As \(Q\) is constant and \(C\) decreases, \(U\) increases.
Final Answer:
D. the electrostatic energy stored in the capacitor increases.

Q29

Two particles A and B of the same mass but having charges q and 4q respectively, are accelerated from rest through different potential differences VA and VB such that they attain same kinetic energies. The value of VAVB is :

a

14

b

12

c

2

d

4

Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

4

Explanation

The kinetic energy (\(KE\)) gained by a charged particle when accelerated through a potential difference (\(V\)) is given by the formula:
\(KE = qV\)
where \(q\) is the charge of the particle.

For particle A with charge \(q\) and potential difference \(V_A\):
\(KE_A = qV_A\)
For particle B with charge \(4q\) and potential difference \(V_B\):

\(V_A = 4V_B\)

\(\frac{V_A}{V_B} = 4\)

The value of \(\left(\frac{V_A}{V_B}\right)\) is 4.
