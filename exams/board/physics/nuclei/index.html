Q2

Energy released when two deuterons (H12) fuse to form a helium nucleus (H2e4) is :
(Given : Binding energy per nucleon of H12=1.1MeV and binding energy per nucleon of H2e4=7.0MeV )

a

8.1 MeV

b

5.9 MeV 

c

23.6 MeV

d

26.8 MeV

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

23.6 MeV

Explanation


Binding energy per nucleon of deuteron \( \left(^2_1\text{H}\right) = 1.1\, \text{MeV} \)
Binding energy per nucleon of helium-4 \( \left(^4_2\text{He}\right) = 7.0\, \text{MeV} \)

Two deuterons \( \Rightarrow \) total nucleons = 2 × 2 = 4

\[
\text{BE}_{\text{reactants}} = 2 \times (1.1 \times 2) = 4.4\, \text{MeV}
\]

\[
\text{BE}_{\text{product}} = 4 \times 7.0 = 28.0\, \text{MeV}
\]

\[
Q = \text{BE}_{\text{product}} - \text{BE}_{\text{reactants}} = 28.0 - 4.4 = \boxed{23.6\, \text{MeV}}
\]

Q7

A radioactive nucleus \(\mathrm{n}_2\) has 3 times the decay constant as compared to the decay constant of another radioactive nucleus \(n_1\). If initial number of both nuclei are the same, what is the ratio of number of nuclei of \(n_2\) to the number of nuclei of \(n_1\), after one half-life of \(n_1\) ?

[JEE Main 2025, 23 Jan (Shift 1)]

a

8

b

4

c

1/8

d

1/4

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

1/4

Explanation

The number of remaining nuclei after time t can be given by:

N(t)=N0e−λt

For half-life t1/2 of n1, we know that:

t1/2=ln2λ1

At the end of one half-life, the number of n1 nuclei left will be:

N1=N02

For nucleus n2, which has a decay constant 3 times that of n1 the remaining number of nuclei will be:

N2=N0e-3λ1t

Since t=t1/2=ln2λ1, we substitute it into the equation for N2 :

N2=N0e-3λ1×ln2λ1=N0e-3ln2=N0123=N08


Now, the ratio of the number of nuclei of n2 to n1 after one half-life of n1 is:

N2N1=N08N02=14

Q9

Which of the following statements is not true for nuclear forces?

a

They are stronger than Coulomb forces.

b

They have about the same magnitude for different pairs of nucleons.

c

They are always attractive.

d

They saturate as the separation between two nucleons increases.

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

They are always attractive.

Explanation

A) They are stronger than Coulomb forces.
True — Nuclear forces are much stronger than electrostatic (Coulomb) forces between protons.

(B) They have about the same magnitude for different pairs of nucleons (n–n, p–p, n–p).
True — Nuclear forces are approximately charge-independent.

(C) They are always attractive.
Not true — At very small distances (below about 0.5 fm), nuclear forces become repulsive to prevent nucleons from collapsing into each other.

(D) They saturate as the separation between two nucleons increases.
True — Each nucleon interacts only with nearby ones; the force has a short range and saturates.

C) They are always attractive.​

Q11

The energy equivalent of 1 g of substance is :

a

11.2×1024MeV

b

5.6×1026MeV

c

5.6×1012MeV

d

5.6 eV

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

5.6×1026MeV

Explanation

Convert mass to kilograms:\(m=1\,\text{g}=1\times 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}\)

Square the speed of light:\(c^{2}=(3\times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s})^{2}=9\times 10^{16}\,\text{m}^{2}/\text{s}^{2}\)

Calculate the energy:\(E=m\times c^{2}=(1\times 10^{-3}\,\text{kg})\times (9\times 10^{16}\,\text{m}^{2}/\text{s}^{2})=9\times 10^{13}\,\text{J}\) Other Units The energy can also be expressed in other units:

Approximately \(2.5\times 10^{7}\) kilowatt-hours (kWh).Approximately \(5.6\times 10^{26}\) mega-electron volts (MeV).
