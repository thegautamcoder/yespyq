Q1

For a diatomic gas:

  • Let γ1=CpCv\gamma_1 = \frac{C_p}{C_v} for a rigid molecule (without vibrational modes).
  • Let γ2=CpCv\gamma_2 = \frac{C_p}{C_v}​​ for a diatomic molecule with vibrational modes included.

Determine the relationship between γ1\gamma_1​ and γ2\gamma_2​.

(Shift II Memory Based)​

a

γ2<γ1\gamma_2 < \gamma_1​

b

γ2>γ1\gamma_2 > \gamma_1​

c

γ2=γ1\gamma_2 = \gamma_1​

d

γ2=2γ1\gamma_2 = 2 \gamma_1​

✓ Correct answer: a)

γ2<γ1\gamma_2 < \gamma_1​

ExplanationKey Concepts:

  1. Degrees of Freedom:

    • A rigid diatomic molecule has 5 degrees of freedom (3 translational + 2 rotational).
    • A diatomic molecule with vibrational modes has additional vibrational degrees of freedom.

  2. Specific Heats (CvC_v​ and CpC_p):

    • The internal energy is proportional to the degrees of freedom. For a rigid diatomic gas: Cv=5R2C_v = \frac{5R}{2}​
    • When vibrational modes are included, the total degrees of freedom increase. Each vibrational mode adds 2R2R, making: Cv=5R2+2R=9R2C_v = \frac{5R}{2} + 2R = \frac{9R}{2}​
    • CpC_p​ is related to CvC_v by: Cp=Cv+RC_p = C_v + R

  3. Adiabatic Index (γ\gammaγ):

    • The adiabatic index γ\gammaγ is defined as: γ=CpCv=1+RCv\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v} = 1 + \frac{R}{C_v}​
    • For the rigid diatomic gas: γ1=CpCv=7R25R2=75=1.4\gamma_1 = \frac{C_p}{C_v} = \frac{\frac{7R}{2}}{\frac{5R}{2}} = \frac{7}{5} = 1.4
    • For the diatomic gas with vibrational modes: γ2=CpCv=11R29R2=119≈1.22\gamma_2 = \frac{C_p}{C_v} = \frac{\frac{11R}{2}}{\frac{9R}{2}} = \frac{11}{9} \approx 1.22
Step 2: Compare γ1\gamma_1​ and γ2\gamma_2​

Clearly, γ2<γ1\gamma_2 < \gamma_1 because the inclusion of vibrational modes increases CvC_v​ more significantly than CpC_p, leading to a smaller γ\gamma.

Final Answer:

(A) γ2<γ1\gamma_2 < \gamma_1​
