Q1

The escape velocity for earth is v. A planet having 9 times mass that of earth and radius, 16 times that of earth, has the escape velocity of:

a

v3

b

2v3

c

3v4

d

9v4

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

3v4

Explanation
  • Escape velocity v = √(2GM/R).
  • Earth: v = √(2GMₑ/Rₑ). Planet: M' = 9Mₑ, R' = 16Rₑ. v' = √(2G(9Mₑ)/(16Rₑ)) = √(9/16) * √(2GMₑ/Rₑ) = (3/4)v.

(NEW NCERT 11th Page No. 135, 136)

Q2

The escape velocity from the surface of the Earth is \(11.2 \mathrm{~km} / \mathrm{s}\). Find the escape velocity from a planet whose radius is 2 times that of Earth and mass is 8 times that of Earth.

a

\(15.8 \mathrm{~km} / \mathrm{s}\)

b

\(22.4 \mathrm{~km} / \mathrm{s}\)

c

\(5.6 \mathrm{~km} / \mathrm{s}\)

d

\(11.2 \mathrm{~km} / \mathrm{s}\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

\(22.4 \mathrm{~km} / \mathrm{s}\)

ExplanationStep 1: Formula for Escape Velocity

Escape velocity (vev_e​) is given by:

ve=2GMRv_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}

where:

  • GG is the gravitational constant.
  • MM is the mass of the planet.
  • RR is the radius of the planet.

For Earth:

ve,Earth=11.2 km/sv_{e, \text{Earth}} = 11.2 \text{ km/s}

Step 2: Ratio of Escape Velocities

The escape velocity of a planet compared to Earth is:

ve,Planetve,Earth=MPlanet/MEarthRPlanet/REarth\frac{v_{e, \text{Planet}}}{v_{e, \text{Earth}}} = \sqrt{\frac{M_{\text{Planet}} / M_{\text{Earth}}}{R_{\text{Planet}} / R_{\text{Earth}}}}

Step 3: Given Data
  • The mass of the planet is 8 times the mass of Earth: MPlanet=8MEarthM_{\text{Planet}} = 8 M_{\text{Earth}}​
  • The radius of the planet is 2 times the radius of Earth: RPlanet=2REarthR_{\text{Planet}} = 2 R_{\text{Earth}}​
Step 4: Substituting the Values

ve,Planetve,Earth=8MEarth2REarth\frac{v_{e, \text{Planet}}}{v_{e, \text{Earth}}} = \sqrt{\frac{8M_{\text{Earth}}}{2R_{\text{Earth}}}}​ =82=4=2= \sqrt{\frac{8}{2}} = \sqrt{4} = 2

​ ve,Planet=2×11.2=22.4 km/sv_{e, \text{Planet}} = 2 \times 11.2 = 22.4 \text{ km/s}

Final Answer:

22.4 km/s\boxed{22.4 \text{ km/s}}​

Q5

Two planet A and B are revolving around a massive star such that \(r_A=2 r_B\) and \(m_A=4 \sqrt{3} m_B\). Find ratio of angular momentum of planet B to planet A.(Shift - II Memory Based)

a

\(8 \sqrt{3}\)

b

\(\frac{1}{4 \sqrt{6}}\)

c

\(\frac{1}{2 \sqrt{3}}\)

d

\(\frac{1}{3 \sqrt{2}}\)

🔒

✓ Correct answer: b)

\(\frac{1}{4 \sqrt{6}}\)

ExplanationStep 1: Understanding Angular Momentum in Circular Orbits

The angular momentum (LL) of a planet revolving around a massive star in a circular orbit is given by:

L=mrvL = m r v

From Kepler’s laws, the orbital velocity vv is given by:

v=GMrv = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}

Substituting this into the angular momentum equation:

L=mrGMrL = m r \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}

​ L=mGMrL = m \sqrt{G M r}​

Step 2: Finding Ratio LBLA\frac{L_B}{L_A}​​

For planets A and B, we have:

  • rA=2rBr_A = 2r_B​
  • mA=43mBm_A = 4\sqrt{3} m_B​

Since LL depends on mm and r\sqrt{r}​:

LA=mAGMrA=(43mB)GM(2rB)L_A = m_A \sqrt{G M r_A} = (4\sqrt{3} m_B) \sqrt{G M (2r_B)}

​ =43mB×2×GMrB= 4\sqrt{3} m_B \times \sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{G M r_B}

​ =46mBGMrB= 4\sqrt{6} m_B \sqrt{G M r_B}

Similarly, for planet B:

LB=mBGMrBL_B = m_B \sqrt{G M r_B}

Step 3: Calculating the Ratio

LBLA=mBGMrB46mBGMrB\frac{L_B}{L_A} = \frac{m_B \sqrt{G M r_B}}{4\sqrt{6} m_B \sqrt{G M r_B}}​​ =146= \frac{1}{4\sqrt{6}}​

Final Answer:

B146\boxed{B} \quad \frac{1}{4\sqrt{6}}​

Q8

Consider a star of mass \(m_2 kg\) revolving in a circular orbit around another star of mass \(m_1 kg\) with \(m_1 \gg m_2\). The heavier star slowly acquires mass from the lighter star at a constant rate of \(\gamma kg / s\). In this transfer process, there is no other loss of mass. If the separation between the centers of the stars is \(r\), then its relative rate of change \(\frac{1}{r} \frac{d r}{d t}\) (in \(s ^{-1}\) ) is given by:

[JEE Advanced 2025]

a

-3γ2m2

b

-2γm2

c

-2γm1

d

-3γ2m1

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

-2γm2

Explanation
  • System: Binary star system with masses m1andm2 (m1≫m2).
  • Process: Mass transfer from lighter star (m2) to heavier star (m1) at a rate γ.
    • m˙1=γ
    • m˙2=−γ
    • Total mass M=m1+m2is constant.
  • Principle: Conservation of orbital angular momentum L.

Step 1: Write the expression for Angular Momentum. The orbital angular momentum L for a binary system is given by: L=μGMr where μ=m1m2m1+m2 is the reduced mass, M is the total mass, and r is the separation.

Step 2: Differentiate with respect to time. Taking the natural logarithm of both sides: lnL=lnμ+12lnGM+12lnr Since L,G, and M are constant, their derivatives are zero. Differentiating with respect to time t : 0=1μdμdt+12rdrdtRearranging to solve for the relative rate of change of r : 1rdrdt=−21μdμdt

Step 3: Calculate 1μdμdt. Using the definition of reduced mass μ=m1m2M: lnμ=lnm1+lnm2−lnM 1μdμdt=m˙1m1+m˙2m2Substitute m˙1=γ and m˙2=−γ: 1μdμdt=γm1−γm2=γ1m1−1m2

Step 4: Apply the approximation m1≫m2. Since m1 is much larger than m2, the term 1m1is negligible compared to 1m2.1μdμdt≈γ0−1m2=−γm2

Step 5: Final Result. Substitute this back into the equation for 1rdrdt:1rdrdt=−2−γm2=2γm2

Note: While the derived physical result is positive (indicating orbit expansion), the options provided in the question all have a negative sign. This suggests a sign convention difference or a typo in the question statement regarding the direction of mass flow (usually Heavy → Light causes shrinking/negative rate). However, the magnitude and dependency on m2 clearly point to Option (B).

Answer: (B) −2γm2

Q10

Satellite A is launched into a circular orbit of radius R. Satellite B is launched into a circular orbit of radius 1.03R. By approximately what percentage is the time period of B greater than that of A?

(Shift I - Memory Based)

a

9%

b

4.5%

c

3%

d

2.5%

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: b)

4.5%

Explanation

By Kepler's Third Law, T∝R3/2T \propto R^{3/2}.

  • Time period ratio: TBTA=(RBRA)3/2=(1.03)3/2.\frac{T_B}{T_A} = \left( \frac{R_B}{R_A} \right)^{3/2} = (1.03)^{3/2}.
  • Using binomial approximation, (1.03)3/2≈1+32(0.03)=1.045(1.03)^{3/2} \approx 1 + \frac{3}{2}(0.03) = 1.045
  • Percentage increase: ΔT%=(1.045−1)×100=4.5%.\Delta T \% = (1.045 - 1) \times 100 = 4.5\%.

Thus, the time period of BB is approximately 4.5% greater than that of AA.

Q12

The mass of the moon is \(\frac{1}{144}\) times the mass of a planet and its diameter is \(\frac{1}{16}\) times the diameter of a planet. If the escape velocity on the planet is v, the escape velocity on the moon will be :

a

\(\frac{v}{6}\)

b

\(\frac{v}{12}\)

c

\(\frac{v}{4}\)

d

\(\frac{v}{3}\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

\(\frac{v}{3}\)

Explanation

Escape velocity:

ve=2GMRv_e=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}}

Given:

Mm=M144,Rm=R16M_m=\frac{M}{144}, \quad R_m=\frac{R}{16}

vmv=Mm/RmM/R=(M/144)(R/16)⋅RM\frac{v_m}{v}=\sqrt{\frac{M_m/R_m}{M/R}} =\sqrt{\frac{(M/144)}{(R/16)}\cdot \frac{R}{M}}

=16144=13=\sqrt{\frac{16}{144}}=\frac{1}{3}

vm=v3v_m=\frac{v}{3}
