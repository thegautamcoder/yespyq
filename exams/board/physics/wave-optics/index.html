Q1

A ring of radius 3 cm has a soap film which is getting evaporated. Light of wavelength \(\lambda=580 \mathrm{~nm}\) gives minimum transmission every 12 s . Find the rate of evaporation. (Refractive index=1.45)(Shift - II Memory Based)

a

\(\begin{aligned}& 1.5 \pi \times 10^{-13} \mathrm{~m}^3 / \mathrm{s}\end{aligned}\)

b

\(\begin{aligned}& 15 \pi \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{~m}^3 / \mathrm{s}\end{aligned}\)

c

\(\begin{aligned}& 3 \pi \times 10^{-13} \mathrm{~m}^3 / \mathrm{s}\end{aligned}\)

d

\(\begin{aligned}& 3 \pi \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{~m}^3 / \mathrm{s}\end{aligned}\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

✓ Correct answer: b)

\(\begin{aligned}& 15 \pi \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{~m}^3 / \mathrm{s}\end{aligned}\)

Explanation

To find the rate of evaporation of the soap film, we need to determine the rate at which its thickness decreases over time.

Step 1: Understanding the Given Data
  • Wavelength of light: λ=580\lambda = 580 = 580×10−9580 \times 10^{-9}
  • Refractive index of soap film: μ=1.45\mu = 1.45
  • Minimum transmission occurs every T=12T = 12
  • Radius of the ring: r=3r = 3 = 0.030.03
Step 2: Condition for Minimum Transmission

A thin-film interference pattern changes when the optical path difference changes by half a wavelength in the medium:

∆t=λ2μ​

This means that the thickness decreases by:

Δt=λ2μ\Delta t = \frac{\lambda}{2\mu}​

Substituting values:

Δt=580×10−92×1.45\Delta t = \frac{580 \times 10^{-9}}{2 \times 1.45}

Δt=580×10−92.9\Delta t = \frac{580 \times 10^{-9}}{2.9}

​ Δt=200×10−9 m=2×10−7 m\Delta t = 200 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m} = 2 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}

This thickness decreases every 12 seconds, so the rate of evaporation per second:

∆tdt=2×10−7 m12​ =1.67×10−8 m/s= 1.67 \times 10^{-8} \text{ m/s}

Step 3: Finding the Volume Evaporation Rate

The evaporating volume per second is:

Rate of evaporation=Surface area×∆tdt=πr2×∆tdt​

Substituting values:

=π(0.03)2×(1.67×10−8)= \pi (0.03)^2 \times (1.67 \times 10^{-8})

=π(9×10−4)×(1.67×10−8)= \pi (9 \times 10^{-4}) \times (1.67 \times 10^{-8})

=15π×10−12 m3/s

Step 4: Choosing the Correct Option

From the given options, the correct answer : 15π×10−12

Q3

A glass slab of refractive index μ0=1.44\mu_0 = 1.44 is coated with a thin film of refractive index μf=2\mu_f = 2. The minimum thickness of the film, so that maximum transmission of green light of wavelength λ = 5000A˚ (incident normally) takes place, is:

(Shift II Memory Based)

a

625A˚

b

2500A˚

c

1250A˚

d

1000A˚

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

1250A˚

Explanation

To achieve maximum transmission of green light, the thin film must cause destructive interference for the light reflected from its top and bottom surfaces. This is a standard case of thin-film interference, and the condition for minimum thickness for maximum transmission (destructive reflection) is:

2 n t = m λ

where: n is the refractive index of the film, t is the thickness of the film, λ is the wavelength of light in the medium of the film, m is the order of interference (minimum for the thinnest film).

Here, m = 1

So, t =m  λ2 n=1×50002 ×2=1250A° 

Q4

In a Young's Double Slit Experiment (YDSE), for a wavelength λ1 = 600 nm, the 10th bright fringe is observed at a distance of 10 mm from the central maximum.For a new wavelength λ2 = 660 nm, what will be the distance of the 10th bright fringe from the central maximum?

(Shift - I Memory based)

a

9

b

10

c

11

d

12

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: c)

11

ExplanationStep 1: Understanding the Fringe Position Formula

In Young’s Double Slit Experiment (YDSE), the position of the nthn^{th} bright fringe is given by:

yn=n×λDdy_n = n \times \frac{\lambda D}{d}​

where:

  • yny_n​ = distance of the nthn^{th} bright fringe from the central maximum,
  • λ\lambda= wavelength of light used,
  • DD = distance between slits and screen,
  • dd= separation between the two slits,
  • nn = fringe order.
Step 2: Finding Dd\frac{D}{d}​ Using Given Data

For λ1=600\lambda_1 = 600, the 10th bright fringe is at 10 mm:

y10=10×600Dd=10 mmy_{10} = 10 \times \frac{600 D}{d} = 10 \text{ mm}

Dd=1600 mm/nm\frac{D}{d} = \frac{1}{600} \text{ mm/nm}

Step 3: Finding y10y_{10}​ for λ2=660\lambda_2 = 660

Now, using the same formula for λ2=660\lambda_2 = 660:

y10=10×660Ddy_{10} = 10 \times \frac{660 D}{d}​

Substituting Dd=1600 mm/nm\frac{D}{d} = \frac{1}{600} \text{ mm/nm}:

y10=10×660600y_{10} = 10 \times \frac{660}{600}

​ y10=10×1.1y_{10} = 10 \times 1.1

y10=11 mmy_{10} = 11 \text{ mm}

Q19

When unpolarized light is incident at an angle of \(60^{\circ}\) on a transparent medium from air, the reflected ray is completely polarized. The angle of refraction in the medium is:

a

\(60^{\circ}\)

b

\(45^{\circ}\)

c

\(90^{\circ}\)

d

\(30^{\circ}\)

🔒
Answer & explanation — PYQ Pass

Unlock this and thousands more solved PYQs.

Unlock · ₹149
✓ Correct answer: d)

\(30^{\circ}\)

Explanation

According to Brewster's Law, when reflected light is completely polarized, the angle of incidence \((i)\) and the angle of
refraction \((r)\) sum up to \(90^\circ\).

\(i + r = 90^\circ\)

\(60^\circ + r = 90^\circ\) \(\Longrightarrow\) \(r = 30^\circ\).
